AURORA R-54 / DUMP RECOVERY AND SNOW CONTACT / 2026-11-05

The operator's failed return pass was reproduced with current saved tuning.
A 1 m3 loaded tank dumped 975 L into nine fixed .22 m kernels, building a
2.88 m wall. The previous driven return accepted only 29.1 L (authored) or
9.2 L (procedural), at the ordinary 1.06 m/s travel speed. Previous narrow
windrow tests did not cover a real dumped-bin return.

Dump snow now settles against the existing field into a finite repose-shaped
pile, across the actual outlet width. Every deposited litre is normalized back
to actual ice cells. Pit samples and off-ice material keep their existing
ledgers. This is a physical distribution change for dumped stock; ordinary
side overflow and its calibrated onset are unchanged.

The deterministic machine samples the real snow heightfield. Deep snow opposes
travel and adds machine load. The reaction is yielding contact: the body can
creep through compressible snow, and a lowered intake limits forward travel
to its available feed rate. Reverse remains available to withdraw. This is
not a Unity Rigidbody or rigid MeshCollider, and wheels do not delete stock.

With moving forward contact and the conditioner fully lowered, deep snow is
fed into the existing trough at finite horizontal-auger throughput and free
capacity. It then uses the existing horizontal-to-vertical-to-tank transport.
The final thin remainder of an engaged deep bank remains rate limited, avoiding
a sudden whole-footprint gulp at the 50 mm threshold. Stopped/raised equipment
does not vacuum the pile. Ordinary shallow layers retain whole-layer pickup.
Stopped/full/jammed transport retains or spills stock through its existing
finite reservoirs, never bypasses them. The actual-delivery bin spout and its
visibility control are unchanged.

The tank reservoir now accumulates in double precision internally. Full-bin
recovery exposed approximately .2 L of float addition drift over thousands of
tiny deliveries. The public float reading and controls remain compatible.
