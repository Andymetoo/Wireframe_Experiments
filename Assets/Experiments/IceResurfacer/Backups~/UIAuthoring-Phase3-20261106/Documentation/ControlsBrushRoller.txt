1. CONDITIONER-MOUNTED WATER VALVES AND BLADE HANDWHEEL
------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Your hierarchy is kept: the blade screw/handwheel assembly and both valve bearings stay children of the
conditioner and rise 340 mm with it.
Root causes (all measured before the fix, at 0/50/100% lift):
  a. Runtime batching merged the handwheel and both valve handles into the conditioner's static mesh
     (0 renderers left under each control). They rode with the conditioner but could never turn or
     highlight. The machine batch excluded moving parts; the conditioner batch did not.
  b. Picking used the generated layout's valve grip height (WaterValveStemHeight, 0.24 m in the handle
     frame); your lengthened stems put the grips at 0.564 m. Clicks on the visible grips hit nothing; at
     0% lift the flood grip selected the blade handwheel. The turning plane was at the same wrong height.
  c. Drag angles were taken between two pointer positions in different frames, so a conditioner moving
     under a held, still pointer would have turned the valve.
  d. A control's forgiving hit margin could take a click from another control's visible part.
Changes:
  - Every moving control keeps its own renderers; only what is rigid on the conditioner is merged.
  - Grips (lever balls, valve grips, handwheel rim and spinner knob, breaker T handle) are measured from
    the authored parts before batching, in each control's own frame. Picking, hover, the turning plane,
    the E view and the Inspector reach advisories all use them. The generated machine's numbers are
    identical to before.
  - Turning uses the control's rest frame; each frame's pointer delta is projected through the control
    as drawn now, so only the hand turns it.
  - The drawn grip under the pointer always wins; margins apply only where no drawn grip is hit.
  - The seat (cushion, back, arm pads) hides a control behind it.
Turning a control seen edge-on (finishing pass). From the seat the valve handles turn in a plane seen
12 deg from edge-on at 50% lift and 1.3-2 deg at full lift. There the pointer's angle about the stem is
ill-conditioned: one pixel of hand jitter swings it by tens of degrees. Measured with a simulated hand
(4 deg steps, up to 2 px of jitter) following the visible grip 48 deg, the old projection turned the wash
valve 37 deg at 50% lift, the flood valve 23.5 deg and the wash valve 66 deg at full lift (controlled
experiment, Evidence/experiment-plane-projection-only-results-Verify.txt). Now, within 15 deg of
edge-on, the handle follows its own visible path: it turns to the point of that path nearest the hand,
keeps turning the way it was going round the ends of the stroke (where both sides of the ellipse lie a
few pixels apart), and drops whatever lies past an end stop, so reversing works at once. Seen more
openly, the exact projection is used as before.
Measured after (Play, authored machine, virtual mouse through the real input path):
  hover + tint + click on all 8 controls at 0/25/50/75/100% lift; no other control within 6 px of a grip;
  45 deg drag sets 0.500 on both valves and 0.0286 mm on the handwheel at every lift; the visible handle
  turns; holding a valve or the handwheel while the conditioner rises 0->100% changes nothing (0.000).
  The same 45 deg in one slow frame: exact wherever the view is 6 deg or more off edge-on (at full lift,
  1.2-1.7 deg off, a single jump round the end of the stroke is ambiguous and is reported, not checked).
  Jittery hand, 48 deg: 44-52 deg on both valves and the handwheel at 0/50/100% lift; each valve driven to
  its open stop and back to closed by the same hand at every lift; the handwheel turned by its knob
  reaches the 3.5-turn stop (0.800 mm), holds it when turned further, and one turn back gives 0.571 mm,
  at 0% and 100% lift.

4. GUIDE ROLLER AND BOARD CONTACT
------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Causes found:
  - Any rejected step whose inward share exceeded 45% of the step (about 27 deg), or whose slide test
    failed, set speed to exactly 0. Held A/D reaches that angle within half a second, then every step
    is rejected: a dead stop until reversing (25 deg with steering, 45 deg, 15 deg at full lock, corners).
  - The simulated body was one 2.13 x 2.95 m rectangle; your drawn front is 1.70 m wide (wheels +-0.85,
    bumper 1.47 m) and 5 cm longer, and the simulated roller sat 199 mm outboard of yours. The machine
    stopped against invisible geometry, and head-on the drawn bumper entered the boards by 30 mm.
  - The conditioner (side plates +-1.066 m, the widest part) was no collider at all.
  - The roller's rotation was speed x time with the wrong turning direction for a left-side roller.
Changes (Core/MovementContact.cs, Simulation.cs, VehicleOutlineConfig.cs):
  - Calibration: CollisionOutline (85-vertex plan outline fitted to every drawn part except the brush,
    roller and conditioner, sampled every 10 cm), ConditionerCollisionHalfWidth/Front/Rear
    (1.066 / 0.365 / 0.575, about the simulated conditioner centre), guide roller at its drawn place
    (x -1.001, z 2.444, r 0.090 after the move below). Empty outline = the original rectangle (launch scene).
  - Response: the step is resolved, not zeroed. Contacts ahead of the rear axle (roller, nose) pivot the
    chassis about the rear axle, as front tyres scrub (up to ContactPivotRate 0.35 rad/s); contacts at or
    behind it (rear, conditioner) slide the rear sideways along the boards (up to ContactScrubSpeed
    0.45 m/s). Only the inward motion is removed; what the tyres cannot absorb shortens the step, so a
    square impact stops and a glance slides. Every accepted pose passes the full probe. Friction from the
    normal reaction: roller 0.03 (rolling), body/conditioner 0.25 (sliding). A pose already inside the
    driving margin may always move out of it, never deeper.
  - Sliding along the boards no longer re-triggers impact sounds; a mistake is counted for a real bump
    (normal speed over 0.25 m/s, 0.6 m/s on the roller), not for rolling on the roller.
  - The roller turns from its centre's travel along the board face, anticlockwise from above (left side).
  - F3 shows the outline actually in force.
