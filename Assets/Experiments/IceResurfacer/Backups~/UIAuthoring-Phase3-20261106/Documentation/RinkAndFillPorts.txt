The boards are 48 in, inside the 40-48 in range, so they were left alone.
The glass is tall around both ends and the whole of each corner arc. On each straight side it steps
down to the side height in one sloped panel between the two existing supports nearest the corner,
so the change lands on a post, not mid-panel (Shots/After-Elevation-West-Side-To-Corner.png).
The edge channel and supports follow that profile.

Service gate (southeast corner)
  Before: every band stopped at 2.732 m from the gate axis as an open, zero-thickness edge, with a
  ~0.28 m gap to the jamb; you could see into the bands.
  After: the bands are closed solids with end caps and shared smooth normals. They end at 2.91 m
  inside a solid board post on each side (2.73 - 2.91 m, 25 mm chamfer) made of:
    Gate post kick plate / Gate post board / Gate post top rail   (kick, enamel and rail terminations)
    Gate post rubber edge guard                                    (vertical rubber edging, 45 mm)
    Gate glass end stanchion                                       (stainless; the glass stops at 2.82 m)
    Gate post glass return + Gate glass return top edge            (glass turns back to the portal upright)
  Hierarchy: Rink boards and ice sheet > Service gate board post / southwest and / northeast.
  Nearest new part to the opening: the edge guard at 2.728 m (opening and collision half width 2.70).
  The machine clears the gate. Collision did not change: no simulation code changed, and Play
  checks the simulated board line.

Collision tradeoff (unchanged, measured)
  The simulated tank-vs-glass contact (TankCollision) still treats the glass as 2.55 m high everywhere.
  Measured in Play (RinkTankGlass): a tank raised beside the east side glass stops at 45% lift
  (contact at y 1.68 m). In that test and at the north end, no visible tank vertex passes the board
  line between 2.55 m and the new glass top (0 mm). Below 2.55 m the worst case is 2 mm, as before.

Performance
  Rink triangles 5,664 -> 8,372 (+2,708). The new parts reuse existing arena materials, so runtime
  batching merges them into the existing batches. Authored scene runtime renderers 244 -> 222 (the
  ports are now batched with the machine). Launch scene unchanged (245).

PERSISTENT WATER FILL PORTS AND TANK GAUGES (Machine/AuroraR54.prefab)
------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
AURORA R-54 / rear axle origin > Water Fill Connections
  Flood Fill Port            [IceWaterFillPort, port = Flood]
    Visual Assembly          decorative only: collar, blue cap, aperture, lugs, labels (TextMesh)
    FLOOD WATER hose attachment  "Hose Connection Socket"; its red +X axis is the coupled nozzle's direction
    FLOOD WATER overflow discharge  "Overflow Discharge Socket"; overflow falls straight down from here
  Flood Tank Level Gauge     [IceWaterLevelGauge, tank = Flood]
    Water level indicator (full)   [IceKeepUnbatched]; its authored size means "full"
  Wash Fill Port / Wash Tank Level Gauge   the same, teal

Created at exactly the generated positions: flood socket machine (1.160, 1.220, 0.050), wash
(1.160, 1.480, -0.550). Hose 5.79 / 6.12 m of 11 m; reach 0.27 / 0.42 m of 2.0 m.
Materials: Machine/Materials/Flood water inlet - blue.mat and Wash inlet - teal.mat.

What you can do (in the prefab, then save)
  Move or rotate a whole port: the socket, overflow and labels go with it.
  Move either socket on its own, for example to put the coupling on your own model.
  Reshape, replace or delete anything under Visual Assembly, or replace it with your own mesh.
  Recolour it through the two materials, or assign others.
  Relabel it by editing the TextMesh text under Visual Assembly.
  Move or resize a gauge or its indicator. The indicator scales from its own base, and its
  authored height is full.

What Play binds to (no hidden positions)
  The hose coupling position and orientation, E/click target, reach test, hose length, routed hose
  and breakaway, overflow spill point, coupling audio and gauge level all come from the sockets.
  Tank capacities, flow (12 L/s), reach (2.0 m) and hose length (11 m) did not change.
  Play makes no copies: when the prefab has exactly one complete port of each kind, nothing is generated.
  The carried nozzle and the live hose stay runtime objects.

Inspector (select a port or a gauge; Scene view gizmos while selected)
  It checks reach from the floor beside the parked machine, and the routed hose against 11 m
  (errors). It warns when the hand would reach through the body, the nozzle points into the
  machine, or the nozzle is obstructed. It also reports where overflow lands.
  Gizmos: socket and click radius, nozzle arrow, overflow arrow, reach line, hose route, the
  machine footprint the hose routes around.
  Gauge: "Keep the indicator unbatched" fix if that marker is removed.
  The machine Inspector's WATER FILL PORTS section selects them.

Hose obstacle review
  The hose previously routed around a fixed generated box (1.38 x 1.88 x 2.84 m). It now uses the
  authored machine's footprint: mesh bounds below 2.2 m plus 8 cm, never smaller than that box.
  Walking, the exit point and the hose-crossing test use the same footprint. For the generated
  machine the route is identical to before.

The launch scene (Scenes/IceResurfacer.unity) has no authored ports. It still generates its own, as
before, plus an overflow discharge point at the old fixed offset.

  adopts an existing "FLOOD WATER fill coupling" / "WASH WATER fill coupling" object if you made one

  It adds 41 transforms under Water Fill Connections and leaves all 578 existing machine transforms
