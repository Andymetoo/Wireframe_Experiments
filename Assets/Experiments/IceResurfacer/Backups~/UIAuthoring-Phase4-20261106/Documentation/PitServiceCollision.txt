AURORA R-54 / PIT CONTACT, ROOM AND FLOOR WATER
28 October 2026

The pit stop was a geometry mismatch, not docking assistance. The previous
ContainsVehicleFloor expanded the 4.0 x 2.4 m pit by 0.12 m. BodyFits rejected a
vehicle sample entering that expanded rectangle; the drawn edge was elsewhere.
Six slow approaches (0, +/-15, +/-30 and 90 degrees) stopped 120.0-123.9 mm from
the outline. The 90 degree approach also received a wrong normal because the
legacy contact calculation used the vehicle centre rather than the rejected
footprint point. Historical measurements: Validation~/OperatorAuthenticityBefore/
Pit-collision-before.txt.

The pit rim is now entirely inside that same rectangle. Its width is 0.10 m and
height 0.12 m. Collision retains only 0.01 m numerical clearance. The six
equivalent approaches stop 10.4-13.6 mm from the rendered outline, including
the travel possible in one fixed simulation tick. Footprint edges are tested
against pit corners between the existing nine chassis samples. No artificial
park pose, approach heading, speed reset zone or forced dismount is involved.
Collision can remove inward movement, and a glancing service contact can slide
along the edge, but it cannot automatically rotate the machine. The existing
guide-roller steering response at rink boards is preserved. Ordinary reverse
clears every tested pit approach without relocation or heading adjustment.

F3 displays the accepted chassis footprint (green), rejected footprint (red),
rendered pit outline (cyan), collision outline (amber), and actual contact
point/normal (magenta). Six LineRenderers and their materials are reused.
The HUD reports current speed, obstacle, rejected position and heading, and the
contact point and normal. A retained contact is labeled as the last contact.
Use TAB + F3 for the compact left-side contact panel: it leaves the actual pit
and footprint outlines visible in the center of the scene. TAB restores the
full machinery/performance details. Native HUD-<resolution>-pit-overlay.png
captures show this inspection layout in the successful final native runs.
Pit-contact-0deg.png, Pit-contact-30deg.png and Pit-contact-90deg.png show the

Raised tank and board geometry
The white dasher used to begin at 0.13 m while the yellow kickplate continued
to 0.17 m. Both emitted the same inner face for 4 cm of height. The white
dasher now starts at 0.17 m; the two faces meet at one edge. No depth bias was
added to hide this overlap.

The animated tank and opening door can extend beyond the static chassis front.
A narrow contact probe now follows the authored 58 degree tank hinge, 105
degree opening door, sloping crown and tapered shoulders, including both door
skins. It tests physical height: projecting above the 2.55 m safety glass is
not the same as intersecting glass, and an elevated outlet may overhang the
0.12 m pit rim. Actual service walls and doorway still obstruct the assembly.
Drive and hydraulic lift both test the candidate pose. A blocked lift retains
the attained height; reverse and safe lowering remain available. Raising is
not a general drive lock. At the deterministic east-board fixture the tank
reaches lift 0.4473 before contact. Reversing physically away permits the same
held command to reach full lift. Full lift at the pit still succeeds.
Runtime validation checks the rendered hinge positions and rotation against
the core model; Raised-tank-glass-contact.png shows this specific obstruction.
This remains a bounded procedural contact model, not a general-purpose rigid
body simulation of every fastener, hose, or flexible component.

Room and water
The room width is 11 m, reduced from 12 m. Its local length remains 14 m, with
the main floor beginning at Outward=1.6 m; the 5.4 m portal, centre parking
pose, pit position and nose-in orientation remain unchanged. Supply and hose
holder moved inward with the negative-Side wall to -4.95/-4.90 m. Both actual
fill connections, walking around the machine, hose-taut recovery, pit dumping
and reverse departure are checked by the service suite.

Floor water remains finite. A fixed 5040-cell field receives water at the
actual source centroid. Reused delta and first-moment buffers transfer small
amounts between neighbours at 5 Hz down a mild authored drain slope. Transfer
segments are clipped to the service floor and cannot cross pit, walls or ice.
The physical drain removes at most 1.4 L/s. Source appearance no longer draws
a disconnected, drain-wide water sheet or an instant line to the drain.
An explicit 20 L test source after 80 seconds holds 0.4716 L on the floor and
has drained 19.5284 L, distributed across 21 nonzero cells. Error is below
0.002 L. This is local floor runoff, not an expensive fluid simulation.

WetFloor.shader darkens concrete with restrained rough highlights and irregular
soft edges. Radius and alpha follow existing liters; shader fragments also
clip at the exact room, rink and pit boundaries so a coarse triangle cannot
paint across a corner. Persistent meshes update only when field revision
changes, at most 5 Hz. The renderer adds no water. Service-floor-source.png
and Service-floor-drained.png use an identical camera and source fixture.
The Unity render check verifies a visible source patch and 55,290 changed
pixels after drainage at that identical camera. It caught missing material
footprint properties during integration: clipping uniforms must be declared
in the shader Properties block for reliable SRP material data. Those vectors
are now explicit hidden properties. The final reviewed image shows a localized
dark shallow patch at the source and faint remaining spots after drainage,
with no floating sheet or wall/pit spill-through.

Accounting: TotalServiceDrainLiters is the historical total discharge counter
(overflow, dropped nozzle and runoff), not a second physical sink. For a
whole-system balance, sum actual reservoir storage, actual ice storage, floor
WaterLiters and Environment.DrainedWaterLiters. DepositedWaterLiters is the
floor's cumulative input counter. Do not add both the discharge counter and
the same stored/drained water again.
