Reference and physical controls
-------------------------------
The locally supplied 526 Operating Instructions, printed page 5-3 (PDF page 48),
and the operator's annotated control-station photograph establish the traditional
hydraulic order. Viewed from the seat toward the inboard bank, it is VERTICAL
AUGER, CONDITIONER LIFT, HORIZONTAL AUGER, DUMP TANK. The actual lever assemblies,
pivots and mouse targets have been relocated; this is not a label-only change.
The two augers remain maintained controls. Lift and dump remain spring-centered
hydraulics while held. The blade handwheel, separate flood/wash valves and snow
breaker remain distinct mechanisms at the operator's right.

The fictional R-54 retains its existing left operator station, seat-mounted eye,
61-degree lens, adjustable seated eye offset and approximately 3.5-turn blade
handwheel. The model is informed by the supplied photographs; it is not a scan or
an exact reproduction of either commercial model. No drive ratio, steering limit,
wheelbase, acceleration or turn-radius change is made by the camera code.

Current inputs
--------------
Hold Q: inspect the left conditioner edge and brush, looking about nine o'clock
(yaw -95 degrees) and 75 degrees down, with a 0.28 m left lean. Board and room-wall
clearance constrain the lean.
Hold E: inspect the physical levers, water valves and blade wheel with a small
0.18 m rearward ergonomic lean and a bearing solved from the actual controls.
Hold C: actual external camera above and behind the whole machine. Reversing
does not reverse the camera. RMB adjusts its orbit while held.
Release Q/E/C: smoothly restore the exact prior normal local orientation and eye
position. Overlapping holds use C > E > Q; a single original snapshot survives
the entire overlapping sequence. Wall clearance still applies if the vehicle
moves while looking away. X cancels inspection and smoothly recenters forward.

RMB: persistent manual seated free-look; releasing RMB exposes the instrument
cursor and keeps that orientation. Deliberate RMB motion can interrupt a return.
R: toggle standing. Space: temporarily stand. Both preserve head orientation.
F: mount/dismount, including directly from chase. On-foot E still interacts and
does not invoke the seated inspection camera. Normal on-foot mouse-look remains.
The lower-right cursor does not automatically move the camera; explicit hold-E
avoids unwanted assistance while operating the controls.

Chase implementation
--------------------
The existing driver camera is detached from its seat parent only during chase;
there is no second simulation or vehicle controller. Its target is body-relative,
with exponential position and rotation smoothing. The segment to the camera is
clipped against the shared rink/room outline and portal height. A retracted lens
looks over the machine instead of settling at seat height inside its body.
No additional rendering pass, reflection probe or per-frame object allocation
is introduced. A camera cannot restore a saved lean through a wall that the
vehicle has approached during inspection; in that case the orientation restores
exactly and the eye translation is constrained to the available clearance.

Wheel geometry and visibility
-----------------------------
Both front wheel pivots use the authoritative steering angle; all four wheels roll
from simulated speed. The 1.370 m track and 1.960 m wheelbase are retained. Tire
section width is now 0.215 m, matching the manual's LT215/85R16 nominal section,
rather than the unsupported 0.300 m visual width. This also updates the tire-based
overlap guide rather than silently changing only the render mesh.

The old broad underframe, full-length lower sill, rub rails and front mudguards
crossed or concealed the swept wheel volume. The chassis is now central, the
body sills/rub rails stop between the axles, and front splash guards sit inboard.
The instrument work lamp previously sat directly on the seat-to-tire-crown ray;
its housing, stalk, diffuser and light move 0.18 m inboard over the dashboard.
That removes a real near obstruction without moving the seat or widening the tire.
The floor/tank bottom has actual tire clearance. The tank's snow mesh begins at
the top of that steel floor (tank-local y=0.085 m), so a small genuine load remains
visible through the inspection opening instead of being buried beneath the floor.
The bumper, lamps and front skin now meet the existing 2.500 m forward physical
envelope. Geometry was adjusted without exaggerating wheel angles.
