AURORA R-54 / SNOW PRODUCTION AND CONVEYOR CONSEQUENCES
28 October 2026

This calibration supersedes the old constant .16 bite fraction above the normal
.35 mm handwheel setting. It preserves the normal operating range, 3.5-turn
wheel, 5 cm ice grid, driving geometry, actual tank capacity, snow expansion and
incremental surface reporting. Snow is never added by the gauge or particles.

Primary reference and model limits

The supplied 526 Operating Instructions English.pdf is the primary traditional
machine reference. PDF page 84, printed 9-5, was checked as a rendered image:
with a new 5-inch blade, four handwheel turns adjust the blade approximately
.08 inch (2.032 mm). That is adjustment travel, not a universal prediction of
ice removed during a resurfacing pass. Blade angle, runners, ice and setup matter.
The manual describes .08 inch below the runners as a heavy cut; it does not
publish the Aurora's bite function, throughput, snow density or compaction model.
Chapter 7 describes visible conveyor delivery, stopping before a completely full
bin plugs the vertical conveyor, and reducing ground speed or cut under excess
load. The optional reverse mechanism is not represented by the prototype.

The 526 capacities are 2.83 m3 physical snow volume / 3.54 m3 compacted rating,
727 L ice-making water and 273 L wash water. The previous manufacturer 546
reference has the same snow ratings and 1.956 m blade, but 738/310 L water.
Aurora retains the 546 water values; this pass does not silently claim a 526
water configuration. See Research.txt for the older 546 provenance and root
OperatorAuthenticity documentation for geometry/reference comparisons.

The simulation stores one equivalent bulk-snow quantity throughout the ice,
trough, conveyors, bin, pit and floor. Shaved solid ice expands by the unchanged
3.1 ratio. The 3.54 m3 stored rating is shown in a roughly 2.83 m3 shell via a
fixed 1.25:1 visual compaction conversion. This is a consistent accounting
approximation, not measured snow density or a granular compaction solver.

Baseline experiment, before changing runtime

Snow-calibration-before.csv records 18 deterministic apparatus measurements:
.15/.35/.80 settings, both augers on, both off, and horizontal on/vertical off,
each on initially worn ice and again on the exact resulting surface.

The fixture prescribes straight conditioner poses over the full rounded sheet
at 1 m/s, with 1.95 m lane spacing and .10 m advance. It calls the real footprint,
surface-processing and conveyor methods on true 5 cm cells. Perimeter poses
deliberately isolate machinery; they are not a physically driven route or
evidence of board clearance. Each second pass starts with empty apparatus
reservoirs and copies the entire first-pass ice state, including deposits. This
isolates repeated cutting after servicing; it is not a simulated dump cycle.
The separately driven scored run and real dumping tests cover those operations.
Both passes are dry, with no fresh flood or wash water.

The baseline maximum effective cut was only .128 mm. It removed .191752 m3 of
solid ice, made .594432 m3 of expanded shavings and picked up .297108 m3 of loose
snow. The resulting .891539 m3 filled 25.18% of the tank. A second dry pass
stored .594390 m3, or 16.79%. Enabled conveyors had no escaped snow and drained
fully; their capacity did not explain the low fill. Initial snow is approximately
.12-.43 mm and its first-pass stock was .297111 m3. No evidence justified
inflating that stock, changing the snow expansion ratio or shrinking the bin.

The old stopped-auger spill was placed at the conditioner center. It repeatedly
re-entered the blade footprint. The maximum first pass therefore recorded
1.041901 m3 of spill transfers despite only .8915 m3 of unique source material.
Transfers can revisit the same material; cumulative spilled or collected volume
must never be counted as a new source or added again to current stock.

Calibration and measured results

At and below .35, effective bite remains setting * .16. Above .35:
  t = (setting - .35) / (.80 - .35)
  actual bite = setting * .16 + (.60 - .80 * .16) * t * t

MaximumEffectiveBladeDepthMm=.60 is an explicit Aurora calibration for operator
review. It is not a manufacturer specification or a literal conversion of the
manual's adjustment travel. The .15/.35 settings still cut .024/.056 mm;
the .80 maximum now cuts .600 mm per complete straight traversal. Overlap and
repeated passages remove more material, including already smooth ice.

The source meters separately expose TotalShavedIceM3, TotalExpandedShavingsM3
and TotalLooseSnowPickupM3. The latter is gross pickup and includes recollected
spills. Current material balance is initial loose snow + expanded new shavings
= surface snow + trough + vertical conveyor + bin + pit + floor. SpilledSnowM3,
TotalSnowCollectedM3 and DumpedSnowM3 are transfer histories, not extra stock.
Independent full scans validate the source meters and current stock. The 18
new cases have maximum balance residual below .000015 m3; before calibration
it was below .000043 m3. These are float accumulation residuals, not permitted
unmetered snow creation. See Snow-calibration-after.txt for every measurement.

Deep-cut load includes an additional term proportional to the extra actual
bite. At 1 m/s, .35 keeps its existing .349 estimated load; maximum load rises
from .7757 to about 1.4837 before tank crowding. The first maximum full-sheet
mean is 1.4848, peak 1.5328. This is the existing approximate mechanical demand
model, not horsepower. Existing acceleration resistance uses that load; nominal
steering, turning radius, normal acceleration response and top speed stay fixed.

Finite snow accumulation, side escape and recovery

The horizontal trough holds .080 m3; the vertical conveyor holds .055 m3.
Their capacities remain .012/.010 m3 per second. Excess horizontal material
splits equally between the two conditioner side exits at the side plates. Each
finite deposit lands just outside the current blade path in a .16 m radius
kernel. Nearby solid boards constrain the deposit inward; the open portal can
receive floor snow. The two emission rates are exactly half of the actual
overflow transfer, and are zero while the trough still has room.

LooseSnowPickupDepthMm=6 replaces the old .6 mm/pass loose-material clearing
limit. Shaving solid ice remains a separate, much shallower process. This lets
the blade gather a windrow without requiring many artificial repeated passes.
Every gathered cubic meter still enters the finite trough and conveyors; if
they cannot handle the incoming material it escapes again. Initial worn snow
is thinner than either limit, so normal first-pass collected volume is unchanged.

SnowTransportCondition exposes independent flags so simultaneous conditions
remain understandable:
- Horizontal OFF: snow stays in the trough, then leaves both low side exits.
- Vertical OFF: the horizontal auger fills the throat; congestion backs up into
  the trough and eventually the same low side exits. No upper discharge occurs.
- Full connected bin: accepted volume stops at capacity; rejected upper
  throughput spills and sustained backpressure eventually latches a jam.
- Jam: conveyor transport stops; further incoming snow reaches low side exits.
- Raised bin: the fixed upper outlet misses the bin. Snow leaves the upper
  chute without falsely building a blockage/jam merely because the bin is up.

Upper rejection originates at the authored fixed downspout (.28,1.78,.75 m
relative to the axle), not a broad box floating across the vehicle. Core and
particles share a bounded path deflected toward the nearest equipment side.
That path approximates the body deflection; it is not per-grain collision.

Three reusable meshes show tank stock, pit stock and actual finite trough
bunching behind the auger grille. Five bounded 360-particle pools show accepted
delivery, upper escape, left/right low escape and dumping. Persistent deposits
live in the existing spatial snow field. No particles create a second material
quantity, and no per-snowflake GameObjects are allocated.

The focused default-capacity test creates .043669 m3 of actual side escape,
finds persistent snow on both sides, then traverses both windrows with blade
out and both augers running. A later empty revisit collects nothing twice.
The complete stock residual is .00000016 m3. Additional checks distinguish all
failure states, enforce board retention and preserve zero-weight kernel cells.
Editor/IceOperationValidation adds control-state-driven motion/cutting captures
before and after recovery; its initial poses are disclosed in Operations-results.

Excessive cutting is not paint damage

The old quality formula divided cumulative actual cut by .16 before testing
nominal thresholds. Applying that formula to the new nonlinear maximum would
misclassify one .600 mm pass as 3.75 mm. Thresholds now use actual accumulated
removal: .80 mm ordinary / .65 mm sensitive. Crossing them reduces uniformity
quality; coverage requirements are unchanged. Two maximum full-sheet passes
produce about .4073 mm mean excess above those local thresholds. No routine
paint penalty exists. A paint model requires a real remaining-ice-thickness
field above paint and is explicitly future work. The simulation also does not
resolve every frozen flood layer as a separate slab; accumulated cut and
retained water are treatment histories rather than an absolute ice-height map.

Validation and performance

SnowCalibrationValidation.Run() covers source accounting, blade mapping,
unchanged normal load, two-sided finite escape, recovery/no double collection,
failure distinctions and board clipping. MeasureFullSheet() is the separate
18-case apparatus calibration. Existing aggregate and driven full-route results
are recorded by the parent validation run. No Unity/native performance claim
is inferred from the apparatus measurements. The final player measurements at
1366x768 and 1920x1080 include actual operation and snow presentation.

Remaining operator questions: whether the .600 mm extreme feels sufficiently
aggressive; whether the retained .056 mm normal bite matches desired routine
removal; how visible snow bunching, spill reach and clearing look at working
speed. These are playtest calibrations, not a claim of validated rink thermals,
snow density, machine power or manufacturer operating performance.
