3. Real surface detail and water covering the towel
The annotated conditioner screenshot guides the visible film across the full
towel. The previous shader gave thin edge film too little visual weight even
where physical water was present; the revised response makes that film clearer.
The field is now 520 x 1200 actual state samples at 5 cm spacing, four times the
previous sample count. Fractional footprint-edge stamps conserve emitted water;
continuous reconstruction smooths their appearance without inventing a wide
water ribbon. The physical blade remains 1.956 m and the towel 2.100 m. A finite
0.120 m fringe per side receives five percent of the same water supply.

The current fixture measures a 2.300 m strong-wet raster and 2.367 m rendered
water-response band across the towel. The rendered measurement thresholds a
GPU shader response mask at 60% display intensity; it is not an objective
perceptual-width guarantee under every reflection angle. Tire-aligned parallel
passes have zero dry/unscored gaps. The earlier straight-pass baseline also had
zero gaps, so the reported foot-wide gap was not reproduced in that fixture.
Evidence: ../Validation~/Ice-calibration-runtime-results.txt,
Ice-water-response-top.png, Tire-overlap-top.png and Tire-overlap-operator-Q.png.

4. What Machine Load means and changes
This is estimated mechanical demand: 13% idle, plus cutting, snow held in the
conveyors, 8%/9% for running augers, and congestion/jam contributions. Existing
physics is unchanged. At 100% load the load multiplier permits 65% of normal
acceleration; snow mass and tire grip apply separately. Load affects engine
sound and warns above 100%; it does not lower configured top speed or impose
a drive lock. Jams depend on actual snow stock and conveyor capacity. It is not
measured horsepower or fuel use. Click the meter or open F3 for the explanation.
Evidence: ../Core/Simulation.cs, ../Presentation/IceDashboard.cs and ../README.txt.

5. Wet film settles over minutes
The exponential freeze constant is now 420 seconds. A fresh film retains
48.95% of its initial liquid after five minutes and 5.74% after twenty. Rendered
wet response follows actual remaining liquid, while liquid plus frozen water
stays conserved. Pickup can remove only liquid and remains capacity limited.
This is a configurable gameplay approximation, not a thermal rink solver.
Evidence: ../Validation~/Ice-visual-refinement-results.txt and
Ice-wet-age-00min.png, Ice-wet-age-05min.png, Ice-wet-age-15min.png,
Ice-wet-age-25min.png. See VisualIceRefinement.txt for the full calibration.

6. Worn ice and meaningful cutting depth
Cells now retain initial and residual gouge depth; seeded wear measures about
0.037-0.145 mm. Actual blade bite reduces that depth. A nominal .35 mm setting
cuts .056 mm effectively with the retained bite fraction. Paired .15/.65 mm
passes leave mean residual depths of .0251/.0006 mm across the same 11,480 cells.
Deeper or repeated cuts remove more grooves but produce more conserved snow,
increase load and retain overcut penalties. Washing cannot erase a physical
groove. Untreated, dry-shaved, freshly wet and settled ice have distinct responses.
Evidence: ../Validation~/Ice-visual-refinement-results.txt,
Ice-wear-before-oblique.png, Ice-wear-after-oblique.png and Ice-wear-after-top.png.

Current player and performance measurements
Serial native HUD runs pass at 1366x768 and 1920x1080 with no measured clipping
or managed errors. A first 1366 run showed automatic-pause behavior during an

cause. The same binary passed both serial reruns without a product-code workaround.

Evidence: ../Validation~/HUD-player-results-1366x768.txt and

An initial 5 cm benchmark exposed costly large-sheet updates; its trace is kept
at ../Validation~/SurfaceServiceBefore/Performance-5cm-before-bounded-updates-1366x768.csv.
Surface reports now maintain incremental totals, checked against an independent
full scan. The regression measures 0.0130 ms per changed report with no managed
allocation; its cache costs about 5.6 MB per field. The 5,000-active-tick core
sample measures 0.2494 ms/tick with no managed allocation. Both are CPU-only
measurements excluding Unity, UI and graphics, and neither is an FPS result.

Ordinary visual aging handles at most 65,536 wet entries per 0.16-second refresh.
A nearly fully wet sheet takes about 1.6 seconds to sweep, compared with the
420-second phase constant. The final runtime test observes at most 1/255 packed
wet-alpha error across ten refreshes, within its 2/255 limit. Full clear, rewet
and exact-invalidation tests pass. Dirty physical changes update immediately.
Unchanged quantized alpha skips the GPU texture upload. Physical phase change,
liquid pickup and water accounting are unchanged by this presentation schedule.
