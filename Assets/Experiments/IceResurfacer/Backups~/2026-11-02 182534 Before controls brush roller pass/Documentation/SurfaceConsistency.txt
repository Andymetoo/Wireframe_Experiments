AURORA R-54 / SURFACE CONSISTENCY / 29 October 2026

Scope
This pass preserves the 5 cm ice grid, material sources, independent controls,
simulated loose-snow capacities, blade calibration and strict treatment/scoring thresholds.
The changes connect the operator overview, liquid optics, deposited snow and
physical material transfers more directly to those quantities.

Survey diagnosis and representation
The old normal map point-sampled 520 x 1200 categorical cells into a small HUD.
Initial snow is .12-.26 mm, with an additional .17 mm in sensitive goal areas.
The strict .25 mm loose-snow threshold therefore marked 69,995 cells white in
the deterministic seed 314 fixture. Random ordinary dust produced static and
the sensitive-region mask produced two hard white circles. These are real
initial quantities; they have not been deleted or reduced for appearance.

The normal overview is now a separate 130 x 300 texture. Each 20 cm pixel sums
the physical areas of sixteen 5 cm cells. Colors blend wet-and-cut, wet-but-uncut,
cut-but-dry, untreated and excluded states. Mean snow depth above .55 mm fades
toward white, reaching full opacity at 2 mm. The initial dust therefore produces
zero heavy-snow blocks; concentrated deposits still show. Overwater receives a
separate area-weighted amber overlay. This is an overview of visible treatment,
not a claim that its colors grant full completion credit.

An uninterrupted 5 cm-wide wet-but-uncut seam contributes 25% of a 20 cm pixel instead of
being randomly selected or omitted. It stays wet/uncut; no automatic cutting
credit is awarded. F2 and the exact-cell inspector retain the 520 x 1200 texture
and original classifications. The existing measured 2.05 m-spaced working passes
still identify true uncut seams under a continuous wider water band. Runtime
validation reads the actual GPU wet-response image at the same physical cells,
checks the area-weighted colors and verifies the water-score count. A physically
wet seam receives water coverage, while cutting remains separately deficient.
In the completed Unity surface suite, the GPU-visible wet response spans 2.289 m
at the measured 60% display-intensity threshold. The physical towel is 2.100 m;
the strong-water raster spans 2.300 m and all retained-water cells span 2.400 m.
These measure different quantities and do not imply a wider 1.956 m shaving blade.
Tire-aligned adjacent passes have zero dry or unscored interior cells. At 2.05 m
pass spacing, 80 truly uncut interior cells remain wet; GPU samples and scored
water coverage agree on their water treatment. The small HUD still cannot give
every 5 cm gap a whole screen pixel; exact hover inspection remains available.

Stationary flood and conservation
Previously stationary liters accumulated at the outlet but could not migrate
between ice cells, and the clipped wetness channel hid depth differences. A
conservative 10 Hz height-field pass now moves excess liquid to four neighboring
ice cells. It processes at most 32,768 active cells per update. Films at or below
.85 mm do not spread, preserving ordinary towel application. Each source can
transfer at most 80% of its above-threshold liquid in one update; buffered deltas
avoid directional traversal bias. Boards retain water. Frozen water does not
move. No new visual water source exists.

Each transfer resolves physical freezing, subtracts source liquid and total
retained water, adds the same quantities to recipients, updates overwater and
quality, then dirties the actual textures and report caches. Frozen defects stay.
The existing finite squeegee/pump can recover still-liquid excess into available
wash-tank capacity. Gross lateral-transfer liters may exceed released liters:
the same water can cross several cell edges. It is a transfer meter, not a sink.

Drying and freezing
The physical 420 s exponential freeze model was updating correctly. The previous
optical function 1-exp2(-12*wetness) made half-depth liquid still appear almost
fully wet. Wet optics now follow pow(actual normalized liquid,.70). At 0/5/10/
20/30 min the same .30 mm initial film has optical wet factors 1/.6065/.3679/.1353/
.0498. Actual liquid depths are .300/.14686/.07190/.01723/.00413 mm. The remainder
is frozen, and total retained water is unchanged. Dry polished smoothness and
frozen ceiling highlights are lower than fresh film, while freshly wet ice
retains its strongest sheen. There is no independent visual drying timer.
The existing 65,536-cell aging budget remains; dirty physical changes are immediate.
The completed Unity comparison samples the same real strip at 0/5/10/20/30 min.
Fresh versus 30 min mean RGB rises from 171.60 to 188.34, a 16.74/255 change, as
dark liquid absorption recedes. Older ice remains smooth and somewhat darker
than unprocessed ice. The retained-water balance passes through the phase change.
The former black shape at the top of these measurement images was the camera's
30 m far plane clipping the arena, not a new machine part. The measurement view
now uses the driver's 180 m far plane and an explicit 16:9 projection, and frames
the physical strip from above. Machinery is hidden only in surface measurements.

Surface refresh work
The first sustained native measurement exposed excessive presentation work as
the real wet region grew. The subsequent presentation optimization preserves
the same 5 cm state/process/depth textures, 20 cm area overview, material values
and 65,536-cell aging budget. It changes invalidation and encoding work only.
Each dirty cell calculates its current liquid phase once. If the aging cursor
visits that same cell in the same refresh, it reuses the calculated phase/depth
and unquantized active-or-expired decision. Expired entries still retire normally
and visited entries still count against the aging budget. Temporary markers are
cleared only for that refresh's dirty entries; ordinary aging adds no full-grid
scan or clear.

Each state, depth, process and exact-map texture now uploads only if one of its
encoded pixels changed. An explicit full invalidation still refreshes every
cell. The overview queues a pixel only when one of its constituent cells changes
an actual cut/water/exclusion/overwater classification or its exact SnowMm.
Queued pixels still sum the sixteen underlying physical cells. Merely moving
water within an unchanged classification no longer repeats those summaries or
uploads an unchanged overview. Byte encoding avoids redundant Color conversion;
physical depths and the independent exact treatment rules are unchanged.

The runtime suite now additionally checks that adding actual water over an
existing snow deposit rebuilds zero snow chunks, that newly dirty wet entries
reuse their calculated phase, and that 4,096 unchanged dirty cells trigger zero
texture uploads, overview summaries or snow rebuilds. A real water-classification
crossing must immediately update its exact area-weighted overview pixel. These
new assertions PASS in isolated Unity run 8. Existing full-sheet aging, immediate
removal, rendered wet extent, pooling, phase and recovery checks remain in place.
The completed native 1366x768 sustained measurement reduces Surface.Refresh from
4.425 to 1.574 ms/frame (64.4%); total CPU work falls from 10.719 to 5.532 ms.
The final 1920x1080 run measures 1.577 ms/frame in Surface.Refresh. Large standing-water
regions still cost more than the earlier feature baseline; see the performance
summary for complete timings and the unchanged-state comparison.

Snow escape and recovery
Finite trough capacity and conveyor throughput are unchanged. Two side emitters
now use actual small mesh clumps with randomized size/motion, gravity, and rates
proportional to actual escape. Each has a fixed 720-particle pool; the other three
snow pools remain 360. Multiple clumps visualize the same transferred stock and
never increase mass. Emission stops with the real escape rate.

Deposited ice snow is reconstructed into sparse 2 m chunks with 10 cm mesh sampling.
Only actual accumulations above 1 mm create meshes; initial dust remains in the
ice shader. Each chunk has 441 reused vertices and 800 possible triangles. Exact
changes in a cell's cached SnowMm update its chunk and neighboring seam vertices;
water-only changes do not rescan or rebuild snow geometry. There is no object
per cell. Heights average actual 5 cm snow depth, without a visual height multiplier.
At the standard 60 x 26 m rink size the chunk array is bounded to 390 entries;
mesh objects are allocated only for actual accumulations.
The existing pickup paths remove that exact SnowMm, so shader streaks, overview,
chunk height and tank/conveyor stock update from the same transfer. Repeated
pickup is still limited by 6 mm loose-snow collection per pass and available
conveyor throughput; a deep drift need not vanish on a single pass.
The completed runtime fixture produces 0.080254 m3 of actual side escape. Return
passes with the blade retracted remove 0.099302 m3 from the surface, including
incidental original loose snow. Tank stock rises, side emissions stop, and the
same snow-height meshes decrease. This is not a claim that recovered stock can
exceed its sources: the recovery includes both the escaped snow and pre-existing
surface snow. Core conservation and empty-revisit tests independently guard
against double collection.

Pit snow now has a broad, irregular rounded surface with upward winding and an
integrated volume-normalized profile. Floor deposits replace repeated pyramids
with one continuous half-metre height mesh, bilinear neighboring bulk density,
small spatial irregularity and triangle-integral normalization to stored volume.
The floor snow mesh rebuilds only when snow stock changes, not on water-only floor updates.
The now-traversable unloading grate also permits walking; the old foot-only pit
edge rejection has been removed. Material still enters the receiver through it.
The pit profile clamps its fractional-power input to zero at the edges, avoiding
NaNs from floating-point sin(PI). Runtime checks verify finite vertices, upward
normals and an integrated pit-mesh volume equal to actual receiver stock. A
separate 0.350 m3 floor deposit renders a 0.350000 m3 triangle integral, 0.3307 m
maximum height and positive normals. Floor bounds now follow growing piles.
Old session snow roots are deactivated immediately before deferred destruction,
so neither new sessions nor synchronous evidence captures retain stale meshes.

The revised tank fill uses the authored shell's taper both along its length and
through its shoulders. Its 1.960 m interior is centered at local Z -1.010 m; the
lower width narrows from 1.488 m at the rear to 1.400 m at the front, reaching
1.200 m at full surface height. A bounded 17-row volume inversion determines the
height; every vertex's width follows its actual local height, including tilt.
The physical envelope is 1.930747 m3. The unchanged 3.54 m3 loose-equivalent stock
maps to it through a 1.833487 compaction ratio. This is an explicit fictional
geometry calibration, not a manufacturer compaction rating. Surface roughness
is a visual approximation; the deterministic stock remains the mass authority.

All 12 runtime mesh-envelope combinations passed: 5%, 50%, 85% and 100% fill at
0%, 65% and 100% tank lift. Actual transformed vertices remain inside the floor,
closed front door, rear wall, side taper and roof. At full load the snow depth is
0.6936 m and the inspection opening remains 0.1174 m above the surface. Progressive
dumping, residual unloading and the empty-surface state also passed.

Limits
The liquid solver represents excess-layer redistribution, not full hydrodynamics,
temperature or slab slope. Conservative coarse snow meshes are a low-pass visual
surface, not granular contact physics. Very thin dust remains flat; player and
machine collision do not respond to each individual snow clump. Human operator
review remains useful for snow intensity, drying contrast and stop-pool appearance.
