AURORA R-54 / MACHINERY ERGONOMICS AND VISUAL AUTHORING
29 October 2026

This report covers the physical-model, camera, lighting and editing portions
of the Operator Ergonomics brief and its six annotated reference images.
The addendum supersedes the earlier request for conspicuous trailer articulation.
The associated collision, surface and final validation reports cover those systems.

Actual controls
The old hydraulic bank used four Z coordinates, .255/.085/-.085/-.255 m.
Its earlier tests checked that ordering, which did not establish a transverse
bank. The new mounting plate is .70 m wide and .25 m deep. Four real pivots
share exactly the same fore/aft coordinate and are spaced .17 m across X:
VERTICAL AUGER / CONDITIONER / HORIZONTAL AUGER / DUMP TANK.
The metal cross pivots, fore/aft lever travel, plates, hoses, moving hit bounds
and animations follow these actual mounts. Spring-return lift/dump and maintained
independent auger state retain the existing simulation controls.

Flood and wash handles now share one transverse manifold, .26 m between their
fixed bearings. Both retain L handles, independent analog flow, physical stops
and the same closed-to-open 90-degree sweep. The blade handwheel remains a
separate 3.5-turn control. Its hub has moved clear of the water handles. Labels
call the resurfacing supply FLOOD rather than ICE WATER.
The E view is solved from the actual extent of the bank, valve grips, blade rim
and breaker handle. It uses the seated camera, with no separate inspection camera.
The regression captures include Camera-preset-E.png and Cockpit-hydraulic-controls.png.

Tank and conveyor
The previous shell center was +.20 m from the chassis center. It is now centered.
The main crown is .875 m above the .780 m hinge, versus 1.025 m previously:
the authored crown is 150 mm lower. Its floor remains at the same physical height
above the front tires. The walls, upper shoulders, crown, window and door are
reauthored using shared dimensions; the tank parent does not receive a Y scale.
The moving door and cylinder clevises use those same dimensions. Raised-shell
collision samples use the same geometric mapping before hinge rotation.

The long over-tank pipe, elbow and downspout are removed. A compact blue upright
stands against the rear tank wall and sends snow through a short horizontal
throat to machine-local (0.450, 1.480, 0.470) m. The rear wall has a real opening,
bounded by tapered panels, at the same location. Its fixed outlet separates from
the tank when the tank lifts. Vertical discharge/missed-bin material therefore
continues to use a physically connected production path, not a hidden pipe or a
decorative replacement. The inspection opening remains an actual hole.

Lowering the shell reduces its available storage envelope. The snow renderer
integrates a tapered interior cross-section instead of letting a full rectangular
snow surface protrude through the smaller roof. Simulated stock remains 3.54 m3
of loose-snow equivalent. Its visual compaction is explicitly the ratio of that
quantity to the revised geometric envelope; it is not a claim of manufacturer
compaction. No stock is deleted, generated, or collected a second time by this
mapping. The final conservative interior envelope is 1.930747 m3, with a 1.833:1
loose-equivalent/physical-volume ratio. Its base narrows from 1.488 m at the rear
to 1.400 m at the front; its 1.960 m length is centered at tank-local Z -1.010 m.
The front clears the closed dump door, and the snow width follows the actual
local surface height when the bin tilts. Layout-results.txt and Operations-results.txt
record the geometry and rendered fit checks.

Wheel spacing and operator placement
The supplied 526 manual's specification table gives 1.37 m wheel track and
2.13 m overall width; its LT215/85R16 tire specification supports the retained
.215 m tire section. The suggested .137 m outward movement per side would create
a 1.644 m track, unsupported by that reference. The visual tires have therefore
not been detached from their actual steering or alignment coordinates.
The centered shell changes the left tire sightline. The actual operator station
has moved .06 m outboard, keeping its platform/rail inside the body envelope and
leaving the seated eye height unchanged. The model still uses .39 m tire radius,
the same steering angle and rolling distance, and the same vehicle turning model.
The centered tank exposed another real obstruction: its former rectangular floor
overhung the tapered side skins and hid the tire crown. The steel floor now follows
the actual .763-to-.720 m side taper, retaining its height and thickness. This
removes the overhang rather than changing track or moving the camera vertically.
The body fold is formed by the adjoining skins; the previous extra 12 mm round
steel rail at that crease has been removed. The colored stripe is a thin panel
following the actual taper, instead of a protruding block across the tire view.
The lower between-axle casing also has real tapered front wheel-well panels:
it retains .925 m half-width at Z .90, then narrows to .720 m at Z 1.45.
The old full-width box blocked the inward-steered tire from the seated sightline.
This changes bodywork, not steering pivots, tire width, or the camera's height.
The brush mount moves forward .20 m through the shared BrushPivotZ parameter;
the simulated contact disc and visible arm use that one position.

Camera and lighting
Hold LEFT SHIFT for a seated rear shoulder glance at -160 degrees yaw and
22 degrees downward pitch. Release restores the exact saved head rotation and
eye offset. It works with W at full demand. Overlapping quicklooks use
C > E > Q > LEFT SHIFT; their single saved normal viewpoint survives overlaps.
Q/E/C, persistent RMB look, R standing, held Space and X recenter remain.
RIGHT SHIFT is now the precision and equipment modifier:
  RIGHT SHIFT + W/S  precision pedal
  RIGHT SHIFT + 1    raise conditioner
  RIGHT SHIFT + 7    lower dump tank
  RIGHT SHIFT + [/]  wash-water valve
Unmodified 1 lowers, 7 raises and brackets adjust flood. Left Shift no longer
changes those machinery commands, preventing the new glance from altering them.

The scene's flat Environment Ambient Color is RGB (214,228,245)/255. This is
the same sRGB value entered into Unity's color picker; RenderSettings/Unity
performs the render-color-space conversion. It is not manually converted twice.
Awake saves prior ambient/fog state and OnDestroy restores it, so loading another
experiment does not retain this experiment's runtime lighting.

Visual authoring workflow
Open Scenes/IceResurfacer.unity, then use:
  Experiments > Ice Resurfacer > Machine Geometry Editor
The window edits the launch component's shared calibration through Unity's
serialized properties and undo system. Available parameters include tank roof,
tank lateral offset, conveyor height, wheel track, brush mount/extension and
operator station positioning. Save the launch scene normally to keep those edits.
Validation rejects incompatible dimensions, including a conveyor outlet that
would intersect the roof or leave the tank side.

Rebuild selectable machine preview calls the same IceWorldBuilder as runtime
with arena creation and batching disabled. Major assemblies and their individual
meshes remain selectable in the Scene view. The window can select the snow tank
or hydraulic bank directly and frame the whole model. Cylinder endpoints are
initialized for the preview's real stance.

The preview is disposable and excluded from scene/build serialization. It is
removed before Play Mode, when the window closes, or when Remove preview is used.
It never opens a different scene or stops Play Mode automatically. Freeform
preview-transform changes are inspection experiments and are discarded on rebuild;
persistent design changes use the shared parameters. A separately maintained
prefab was deliberately avoided because its transforms could drift from collision,
material transport and runtime batching. This is the lower-risk editing workflow
requested by the brief, rather than a full asset-pipeline replacement.

Validation status
The final integrated Unity run (Operator-ergonomics-unity-8.log) passes all suites
with zero managed errors. This includes 138 camera checks, 41 physical-control
checks and 92 input/drive-recovery checks. The real Input System exercises the
transverse hydraulic bank, independent quarter-turn valves, handwheel, exact
Left Shift return and Right Shift precision/machinery bindings.

Actual seated upper-tread visibility is 16/39 rays at full left steering, 10/39
straight and 9/39 full right. All three retain the existing six-ray minimum.
Track, tire section and eye height were preserved; the improvement came from
the real floor, trim and wheel-well corrections described above. Both front
tire swept volumes clear the machine at all three steering angles, and wheels
roll using actual travel. The standing inspection opening exposes snow on 12/25
sampled rays. E exposes the blade hub, all 8 sampled rim points and all four
hydraulic grips without obstruction. Q includes the conditioner outer edge and
the forward brush in the same seated frame.

The unbatched authoring preview successfully generates over 100 individually
selectable meshes, preserves the four shell skins and separate tapered floor,
and responds to changed roof, wheel-track and brush parameters. These are checks
against the actual shared builder, not a second preview implementation.
Executed outcomes are in Layout-results.txt, Camera-results.txt,
Physical-controls-results.txt, Authenticity-results.txt and Operations-results.txt.
All screenshots listed above are captured from Unity's actual cameras. Native HUD
and performance measurements are separate from these machinery checks; consult
the final experiment report for their completed outcomes and measurement limits.
Subjective reach, lever comfort, seat clearance and the compact conveyor silhouette
still benefit from an experienced operator's review.
