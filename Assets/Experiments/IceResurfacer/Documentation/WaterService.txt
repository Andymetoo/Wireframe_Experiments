AURORA R-54 / SOUTHEAST SERVICE ROOM AND OPERATOR FREEDOM
28 October 2026

Normal scored and practice sessions begin with 738 L of ice-making water and
310 L of wash water. The independent reservoirs and supply balance are retained.
REFILL PRACTICE explicitly starts a fresh practice session with partial tanks.

OPERATION
F leaves the operator station in Free Simulation anywhere, even on ice, moving,
with the tank raised, conditioner down, or other machinery running. Controls and
actual equipment positions persist. The released pedal allows hydrostatic coast;
unsafe operation is not silently corrected. The exit position is chosen beside
the left step, then a clear rear/opposite-side position if a wall blocks it.

On foot, move with WASD and look with the mouse; RMB is not required. E or click
interacts with the object under the center view. Pausing releases capture; F near
the left step or accessible rear platform boards and restores seated head view.

Recommended refill procedure:
1. Park centered in the marked southeast room space, nose toward the snow pit.
2. F, then walk around the rear to the water station on the machine's right.
3. Aim at the wall nozzle and E to take it.
4. Approach the labeled right rear ICE WATER or WASH WATER neck and E to couple.
5. E at the wall supply opens flow. Watch the independent gauges and repeater.
6. E closes supply. E at the connected neck uncouples it.
7. Repeat for the other reservoir if needed. Face the highlighted holder and E
   to return the hose; a 2.65 m reach and broad aim tolerance avoid pixel hunting.
8. Walk to the left step or rear operator platform and F to board. Back straight
   onto the ice with S; drive forward into the room to approach the snow pit.

The service HUD reports current routed length out of 11 m and TAUT near the
limit. An aimed fixture outside interaction reach names its distance and required
reach. Walking into the machine, walls, pit rim or hose limit names that specific
obstruction. The port labels say E INTERACT; the live prompt distinguishes
connection from disconnection. An upward glance can move the held nozzle farther
from the reel, but steps that reduce an already excessive route remain allowed.
There is no need to reset a session to get back toward hose slack.

Free Simulation permits pressurized uncoupling or returning a running nozzle.
The supply keeps flowing onto the floor until the operator actually closes it.
Boarding with a carried hose leaves the nozzle on the floor. E picks it up again.
Driving while connected is permitted: as routed hose length exceeds 11 m, the
breakaway coupling releases, leaving a loose nozzle and warning. Wall supply
continues spilling. This is a recoverable fictional coupling, not a claim that
an actual Model 546 contains a drive-away safety release.

Guided Training alone labels and enforces safe service/dismount restrictions.
These checks do not affect scored or practice Free Simulation.

REACH DIAGNOSIS / 28 OCTOBER NOSE-IN REFINEMENT
Previously, turning toward the pit left the reel across the machine from the
fillers. The floor hose routes around the solid footprint, so straight-line
distance alone understated its required length. Reproducing the former pit
approach (Side=-2, Outward=8.1, nose toward pit) with the actual six-node route
geometry gives the following values, including vertical end segments:

Layout and machine pose                         Ice-water route  Wash route
Former wall, former pit approach                 13.40 m          13.05 m
New wall, centered default park                   6.28 m           6.61 m
New wall, centered pit approach Outward=8.1        6.85 m           6.84 m

These are numerical geometry reproductions, not measured player frame times or
proof of the user's exact prior pose. The former approach exceeded the physical
11 m hose and the walking constraint could therefore resemble an invisible wall.
Both current service approaches have slack without standing inside the body.
The hose remains 11 m; neither fill reach nor body collision was disabled.

A second reproducible trap came from head movement: looking up could lengthen
the held-nozzle route beyond 11 m, after which the old walking test rejected even
steps back toward the reel. The new test accepts a shorter route while extended,
so the operator can retreat normally. The persistent meter and obstruction
message distinguish genuine hose tension from the visible machine/wall bounds.

CALIBRATION AND ACCOUNTING
The fictional wall station delivers up to 12 L/s, accelerated for practice;
11 m hose length, 1.8 m/s walking, 1.68 m walking eye height, 2 m ordinary reach,
and 2.65 m holder reach are gameplay calibration values. Tank capacities retain
the previous reference values. The wall handle uses full closed/open endpoints,
while the core accepts partial opening. This is not a manufacturer fill rate.

Supplied, stored, overflow and discharged liters are accumulated independently.
Capacity clamps actual tank gain. Disconnected supply and overflow remain in the
spatial environment accounting. The floor displays persistent water deposits;
non-pit dumped snow similarly remains at actual environmental cells. Already
supplied water is never credited to both tanks. The float tank state introduces
small rounding tolerances; cumulative totals use double precision.

IMPLEMENTATION AND PERFORMANCE
The hose uses six reusable visibility-graph nodes around the machine footprint,
a grounded route, and a raised segment to the selected neck. It cannot stretch
through the body. This is geometric routing, not a rope/elasticity solver.
Two fixed environment meshes render tracked accessible floor cells at 5 Hz when
material state changes; no objects are created as snow or water accumulates.
Overflow retains one 160-particle shared-mesh droplet pool. Three synthesized
audio layers distinguish filling, spillage and coupling. The optional CPU sample
covers presentation work only, not GPU rendering or total application frame time.

Remaining operator checks: subjective eye height, hose weight/reach feel, fill
