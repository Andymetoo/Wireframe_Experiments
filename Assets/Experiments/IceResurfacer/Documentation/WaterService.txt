AURORA R-54 / WATER SERVICE

Normal scored and practice sessions begin with 738 L of ice-making water and
310 L of wash water. These are independent simulation reservoirs. The explicit
refill-practice option starts a fresh practice session with partially empty tanks;
it does not change normal starts or refill a scored machine automatically.

OPERATION
1. Stop inside the service bay. Retract the blade, raise the conditioner, stop
   both augers, brush and pickup pump, close wash/flood valves, lower dump tank.
2. Press F to step down at the machine's left boarding step.
3. Walk with WASD; hold RMB to look. Releasing RMB keeps the viewed direction.
   Walk around the back of the machine to the water station on the right wall.
4. Aim at the wall nozzle and press E (or left-click) to take it.
5. Carry it to a labeled right-side ICE WATER or WASH WATER fill inlet. Aim at
   that coupling and press E. Each inlet fills only its own tank.
6. Return to the wall supply handle; E opens it. Watch the repeated tank levels
   and the physical sight tubes. E closes the wall supply.
7. With the wall valve closed, E at the connected inlet uncouples the nozzle.
   Repeat for the other inlet if needed, then E at the wall holder stows it.
8. Return to the left boarding step and press F. WASD resumes normal driving.

E remains the blade-depth increase shortcut while seated; on foot it is context
interaction. F is safe dismount/board. A connected hose prevents travel and gives
specific close/disconnect/return instructions. Carrying a hose prevents boarding
until it is stowed or connected. Boarding while coupled is allowed but travel
remains inhibited, and F permits recovery. Pause freezes filling and foot motion.

CALIBRATION AND MATERIAL ACCOUNTING
The fictional station supplies 12 L/s at full opening, deliberately accelerated
for practice: about 61.5 s for an empty 738 L tank and 25.8 s for 310 L. Wall E
uses fully closed/open endpoints; the underlying supply model supports partial
flow. This is a bay-specific assumption, not a claim about a real Zamboni fill
rate. The two capacities preserve the established prototype calibration.

The core updates at the existing 0.02 s fixed simulation cadence. Supplied liters,
actual stored gain, overflow liters and drain discharge are recorded separately.
At capacity, overflow goes to the floor/drain instead of increasing tank storage.
An open but disconnected hose drains its supply and does not fill either tank.
The float-valued tank state introduces only sub-0.1 L numerical tolerance in the
multi-tank validation; totals use double accumulators. No supply is charged to
the resurfacing-consumption counters.

GEOMETRY, INPUT AND PERFORMANCE
The 11 m procedural hose stores reserve on the existing wall reel. Its floor
route uses a fixed six-node visibility graph around the machine's expanded
footprint; a raised final segment reaches the coupling. Carry movement rejects
positions beyond the available route length. A hanging tail remains visible
when the nozzle is stowed. This is a bounded geometric hose, not rope physics.

The on-foot camera is grounded at 1.68 m, bounded inside the service bay, blocked
by the machine/conditioner footprint and kept out of the dump pit. World target
selection uses the same camera ray as the visible nozzle/handle and a 2 m reach.
Input mode transitions preserve the seated camera mount and head orientation.

Hose routing reuses buffers. Overflow uses one 160-particle pool with a shared
rounded droplet mesh, a short water stream and bounded localized wet geometry.
The mesh is obtained once during initialization, not created per particle/frame.
Three original synthesized audio
layers distinguish tank filling, drain overflow and mechanical coupling. These
are state-driven procedural sounds, not recordings of a real machine.

VALIDATION
Validation~/ServiceValidation.cs covers normal starts, independent capacities,
safe dismount, wrong port/reach/length rejection, disconnected flow, partial wall
opening, pressurized coupling prevention, fill/overflow conservation, departure
interlock and full recovery. It runs in the deterministic .NET suite.

Editor/IceServiceValidation.cs drives real Input System F/E/WASD through the
on-foot loop, walks around the actual initial machine to the wall and both
inlets, fills and overflows, returns the nozzle, reboards and resumes travel.
It emits Service-results.txt and four service screenshots when explicitly run
by the Unity integration validator. Consult those generated results for the
current run status rather than treating this implementation description as
proof that the runtime suite has executed.

OPERATOR CALIBRATION STILL NEEDED
Walk speed, hose reach/weight feel, handle reach, fill acceleration, audio timbre
and relative levels require human operator playtesting. Hose routing does not
simulate dynamic self-collision or every small fitting. The wet floor is a local
visual discharge representation, not a fluid solver; liters remain accounted in
the deterministic model. There is no animated human body.
