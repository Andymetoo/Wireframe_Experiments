Measured geometry and the operator's tire cue
The source already used 1.370 m wheel track and 0.300 m tires: an outside
tire edge is 0.835 m from the vehicle centerline. The blade is 1.956 m and
the water towel is 2.100 m. These dimensions have not been enlarged. A
second straight pass whose inside tire exterior follows the preceding
continuous physical towel edge at 1.050 m has 1.050 - 0.835 = 0.215 m of
nominal towel overlap. This ideal continuous-edge reference differs from
following the rasterized strong-wet boundary measured below.

The captured before implementation produced a 2.000 m strong wet band at
the tested grid phase. Its correctly tire-aligned second pass also had zero
dry or unscored cells between pass centers. The reported one-foot gap was
therefore NOT reproduced as a straight-pass width defect. Camera sightline,
choice of visible boundary, trailer articulation and operator alignment
remain relevant human tests; the measurements do not disprove that feedback.
See Validation~/Ice-width-before-results.txt and IceWidthBeforeProbe.cs.

The new finite film has a maximum 0.120 m lateral fringe per towel side.
Five percent of the source distribution feeds a tapered fringe, with the
remaining supply in the physical towel footprint. It is the same water,
not extra water or free coverage. The boards clip/retain that fringe and
concentrate its available volume on the ice; no non-ice cell receives it.
Off-ice release is separately deposited into the spatial floor runoff map.
This is a conservative lateral distribution approximation, not a fluid solver.

At the repeatable test's 0.100 m grid phase, the new strong wet band is
2.200 m (+10% versus the before test), while the low-volume fringe's full
raster is 2.400 m. Raster cell extents may exceed the 2.340 m analytic film
width by one sample: physical selection is by cell center. The second
center is 1.935 m; its inside tire edge at 1.100 m exactly follows the first
strong wet edge. The measured configuration's nominal towel overlap is
2.100 - 1.935 = 0.165 m. There are zero dry cells and zero unscored cells between
the pass centers. Strong wet means at least 0.08 mm retained water; full
film means at least 0.001 mm. A visible fringe is not a separate cut credit.
The close-board pass reaches the boundary within the 0.100 m sample grid,
and all non-ice cells remain dry. Physical blade and body dimensions stay
unchanged. Actual affected cells are rendered; there is no cosmetic wide trail.

Shaving, water, finish and scoring
The configurable ShavingPerimeterExclusion is 0.1524 m (six inches). The
rounded rink has 149804 ice samples, of which 146888 are in the shaving mask.
Combined coverage and separate shaving coverage use that reachable mask.
Water coverage, water distribution, overwater and surface quality still
measure the entire ice sheet. An untouched excluded rim is not automatically
declared finished. F2 cycles normal ice, the shaving mask, and actual
cut/wash/flood channels; the minimap follows the same diagnostic selection.

The breakdown is coverage 40 points, whole-sheet finish 30, water
distribution 10, avoiding excess 10, material efficiency 5, and disposal/
safe return 5. Actual misuse deducts 1.2 per recorded fault, capped at 30.
Material efficiency compares useful retained film with water consumed; it
does not test a mandatory route or forbid repeat passes. Practice still
calculates conditions while displaying no score.

Liquid pickup and material balance
FloodMm now retains both phases. LiquidMm and its update time describe the
remaining liquid; FrozenFloodMm is material that has frozen when resolved.
Time changes phase without deleting stored water. Freezing is still the
prototype's exponential temperature-free approximation, resolved lazily.
The pickup contact region is the real 1.956 m blade span by a 0.320 m
squeegee strip at conditioner-local z=-0.120 m. Fresh flood water lands at
the separately authored trailing towel (z=-0.440 m, length 0.270 m), behind
the pickup. The pump cannot immediately recycle new flood water while
travelling forward. It works only with the conditioner contacting the ice and
a primed, functioning pump. Fresh wash return and existing surface water
share a 1.45 L/s calibrated pump limit. A 0.035 mm film remains behind.
Wash tank free capacity limits recovery; a full reservoir cannot erase water.
Recovered surface water enters the independent wash tank, with the existing
filter/recovery factor limiting collection. No fresh flood tank is credited.

Overwater defects keep their frozen portion. Liquid recovery removes only
the recoverable portion of a defect, and recomputes actual surface quality.
Waiting alone does not erase an overwater penalty. Frozen water cannot enter
the pickup reservoir. A core regression drove back over a patch and recovered
3.757 L in five seconds; ice mass loss and wash tank gain agreed within
0.003 L. The sum of cell overwater depth fell from 2012.32 to 1674.13 mm
only after that recovery.
Those sums are diagnostic integrals across samples, not one physical depth.
Editor before/after screenshots use a smaller patch and hide machine geometry
for the measurement capture so the finite pickup strip and retained film
underneath the equipment are visible in the actual-cell display.
At the open gate, runoff is deposited after the tick's pickup has used its
sampled availability, so fresh spill cannot inflate the pump or tank-capacity
budget. A near-full wash-tank regression checks this mixed ice/floor case.

Board brush
The arm extends from (-0.90, 0.38, +0.50) in machine-local coordinates with
a 0.38 m arm and 0.25 m brush radius. Retraction swings 85 degrees. Its actual
world center is constrained inward by board contact so the disc does not
penetrate the kickplate. Only cells inside the deployed brush disc lose
snow, which is moved into a local windrow in front of the conditioner.
It does not award cut coverage or teleport material into a tank. Running
the brush with the conditioner raised or outside the rink remains allowed.

CPU work and limitations
The old surface update regenerated every channel of all 156000 texture
samples on each survey update, even when the sheet was unchanged. The new
field records changed cells in fixed arrays. Cut/snow/finish/process/map
channels refresh only for those cells; wet fading touches only active wet
cells using a precomputed interpolation table. Unchanged process and map
textures skip upload. Surface report totals are cached by physical revision;
elapsed time, handling and misuse remain live. There are no per-cell objects.
Brush removal invalidates those totals even when its windrow lands on the
floor. Zero-weight spill-kernel samples do not alter untouched surface quality.
Raw cell edits made by explicit fixtures must call Ice.MarkChanged and
Surface.Invalidate; ordinary processing and deposits track changes directly.

Validation~/Ice-calibration-results.txt records core timing and zero managed
allocation over 5000 fixed ticks. That CPU-only figure is NOT FPS and excludes
Unity, IMGUI and rendering. Use the before/after player performance reports
for whole-frame measurements. Operator feel, exact wet-boundary recognition
from the seated reference, tilted turns and analog water settings require
actual operator playtesting despite deterministic straight-pass success.
