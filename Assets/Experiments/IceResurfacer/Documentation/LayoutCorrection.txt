AURORA R-54 / LEFT OPERATOR, READABLE HUD, WORKING-WIDTH CORRECTION
25 October 2026

Historical correction record. OperatorRealism.txt (26 October) supersedes the
camera release behavior, lens, water width, cell size and snow calibration.

Physical assembly
The actual station is rebuilt at negative local X, facing local +Z. The seat,
pedestal, non-slip platform, column shaft/support, steering wheel, dashboard,
pedal backing and pivoting pedals, valve bank, handwheels, boarding steps and
grab rails all move with it. CameraMount is a child of the seat assembly at
(-0.76,1.90,-0.48) in vehicle coordinates; it is no longer an independent
right-side camera anchor. Rear/right head look includes a +X shoulder lean.

The snow-tank shell and engine compartment move to positive X so the body sits
predominantly to the seated operator's right. Filler/exhaust and surrounding
panels follow the revised layout. This is positive-scale, explicitly authored
geometry: no whole-machine mirroring or negative-scale reflection is used.

The board brush stays on the left. Conditioner centre, trailer tracking,
horizontal-flight handedness, vertical-flight direction, steering signs and
world-space drive/collision geometry remain unchanged. The vertical elevator
is translated from X=-0.28 to X=+0.28 to clear the station, with a connecting
transfer throat and delivery chute; its geometry and rotation are not mirrored.
Physical auger levers and pedals now animate from their existing commands.

A manual snow-breaker rod/guide/T-handle is reachable inboard/right of the
seat. The momentary HUD button or key 6 performs a down-and-return stroke.
The optional core operation assists up to 0.004 m3 from the existing horizontal
buffer into available vertical-buffer space. It conserves material and requires
a lowered conditioner/tank and the vertical conveyor running. It cannot empty
the tank, create capacity, or make snow disappear. This volume and the 0.18 m
visual stroke are prototype calibration assumptions. Existing automatic
conveyance and complete-run requirements do not change when the control is unused.
Mechanism reference: https://zamboni.com/auto-snow-breaker/

Working width: measured rather than inflated
Manufacturer reference: https://zamboni.com/wp-content/uploads/specs/546_specs.pdf
The published blade is 1.956 m / 77 inches; vehicle width is 2.13 m / 84 inches.
Inspection found the original core footprint AND blade mesh already 1.956 m.
A 25% physical increase would be 2.445 m / about 96.3 inches, exceeding the
reference vehicle width. The physical blade/processing width is therefore kept.

The builder now derives blade, towel, guard, side plates, manifold/nozzle
positions and conveyor spans from SimulatorConfig.ConditionerWidth. The towel
is 1.956 m (previously 1.94 m); housing/side enclosure is 2.076 m (previously a
blade-width housing); visible water emission spans 1.956 m (previously 1.6 m).
The 0.2 m grid samples cell centres, producing 1.8-2.0 m cell-band extents
depending on lateral alignment. New tests verify this bound and verify that
cut and wet extents agree. During turns, processed cells stay in the actual
articulated footprint. No texture-only dilation, snow shortcut or score bonus
has been introduced.

To address the narrow apparent path while retaining correct real dimensions,
the seated lens changes from 76 to 64 degrees. At equal distance/orientation,
tan(76/2)/tan(64/2) is about 1.25: the whole physical scene, including blade and
its real treatment path, appears about 25% wider. New seating, clearance and
shoulder position also improve direct inspection. This is a view calibration,
not a claim that the blade physically became 25% larger.

HUD
The existing palette, instrument groups, dials, levers, survey and service
workflow remain. The rack is widened/padded; secondary text rises from 12 to
16 virtual pixels, body from 16 to 19, headings/buttons from 13 to 17. Blade
heading, wheel, numeric cut and hint occupy separate rows. The 1600x900 design
canvas scales to both requested resolutions without hiding equipment controls.
Dial spokes and survey arrows now rotate in local canvas coordinates, fixing
the previous scale-dependent drift across labels and the world view. The footer
has a dark backing, and modal panels use opaque navy to prevent underlying
survey text from showing through the operator guide.
Actual renders and mouse-event checks are performed in a temporary development
player through its native OnGUI and framebuffer. A text-metric audit detects
clipping alongside visual inspection. See Validation~/HUD-player-results.txt,
HUD-layout-issues.txt, Layout-results.txt and captures for
the final observed result; subjective ergonomics still benefit from playtesting.
The mouse checks use messages only to that player's own invisible window, with
no OS cursor movement or activation. Optional wheel scrolling is preserved;
handwheel/valve dragging is the automated continuous-input path.

Preservation
The pure simulation regression is 19 passing scenarios, including the original
full scored route: 95.06% coverage, 90.6/100, zero mistakes, proper exit and dump.
Rink, drive dynamics, scoring weights, audio and existing controls are retained.
All source changes remain inside this experiment. Validation runs in an isolated
copy because the user's repository is already open in Unity. No project-wide
settings or other experiments are edited; no commit or push is performed.
