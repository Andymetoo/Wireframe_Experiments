AURORA R-54 / CONDITIONER, UNLOADING GRATE AND PHYSICAL CONTACT
29 October 2026

Cause and correction
The previous snow pit was explicitly removed from ContainsVehicleFloor, and
ProbeVehiclePose separately rejected every body edge intersecting its rectangle.
Reducing the extra clearance to 1 cm made the barrier agree with its rim but
could not make the dumping area drivable. Direct rejection set vehicle velocity
to zero. This was the reported abrupt stop, not a docking/parking state.

The receiver is now a visible load-bearing grate, flush with the service floor.
ContainsPit still identifies where falling snow enters the receiver; it is no
longer a vehicle or walking exclusion. Room walls remain physical boundaries.
The machine can drive across the grate at any clear approach angle. Its far
wall still limits travel where the real body reaches that wall. There is no
pose/heading snap, automatic parking or new control restriction.

Water reaching the grate enters the accounted drain sink. GrateDrainedWaterLiters
is a subset of Environment.DrainedWaterLiters, not a second amount to add to the
water balance. Deposited snow retains its existing PitSnowM3 receiver stock.
No open fall/drop or tire suspension simulation is claimed for the new grate.

Conditioner mounting
The previous free-trailing heading had a +/-0.9 radian (51.6 degree) limit, far
more than the close-mounted lifting linkage shown in the operator references.
The same hitch and drawbar positions now constrain relative yaw to +/-2 degrees.
This is an Aurora compliance calibration, not a published manufacturer limit.
The actual rendered heading, blade, water outlet, pickup and snow exits all use
the same Conditioner state. Hydraulic lift remains independent of this yaw.
No chassis wheelbase, steering rate, maximum steering angle or traction equation
was changed by this correction. Comparing zero-yaw and 2-degree-yaw fixtures
gives identical chassis positions/headings for every tested step. Full-lock
kinematic rear-axle radius remains 4.4850 m at the established steering angle.

Physical contact audio
The movement solver records actual rejected physical contact points, inward
surface normals and the velocity of that point, including chassis rotation.
Severity is clamp(inward normal approach speed / 1.8 m/s, 0, 1). Total speed is
not used as a substitute for normal impact speed. No vehicle mass or collision
impulse is invented for this kinematic model.

Three reused spatial voices play original synthesized dull board/wall impacts
or the softer guide-roller rattle. They are positioned at the contact point.
Body impacts have a 0.40 s cooldown, roller impacts 0.28 s, and approach speeds
below 0.035 m/s do not create new impacts. The existing roller loop is now spatial
at its actual roller and remains quiet during tangential rolling. Braking,
training restrictions and other nonphysical command refusals do not emit hits.
The clips are intentionally dull and short, with a restrained secondary body
vibration. Final perceived volume and timbre remain an operator listening check.
