AURORA R-54 / VISIBLE ICE, STAMP RESOLUTION AND DEPTH CALIBRATION
28 October 2026

This refinement supersedes the grid, wet-aging and scratch-removal portions
of IceCalibration.txt. The machine's blade and water towel remain 1.956 m
and 2.100 m wide. Water still has a finite 0.120 m fringe per side supplied
by five percent of the same emitted volume. No cosmetic wide trail or extra
water is added. The apparent narrow band was not a mismatched default UV
rectangle: the earlier response gave thin edge film too little visual weight.

Resolution and the actual stamped area
CellSize now defaults to 0.050 m: 520 x 1200 actual state samples over the
60 x 26 m rink, rather than an upsampled 260 x 600 field. There are 599224
ice cells, including 589736 inside the six-inch shaving mask. The grid remains
configurable in SimulatorConfig; current validation permits 0.05 through
0.50 m cells. Changing calibration starts a new session.

The flood footprint uses four area samples in boundary/fringe cells and a
constant-density shortcut in fully interior cells. Partial boundary cells
therefore receive partial quantities instead of a full binary stamp. All
sample weights are normalized against the one finite emitted supply; volume
is conserved. The renderer reconstructs those real data continuously and
gives a visible response to thin liquid film across the full towel width.
The fixed spatial limits still apply and no non-ice cells receive flood water.

The deterministic parallel-pass fixture now measures a 2.300 m strong wet
raster and 2.400 m low-volume film raster at its particular grid phase.
The second vehicle center is 1.985 m from the first; its tire exterior at
1.150 m follows the preceding strong wet boundary. Nominal towel overlap
is 2.100 - 1.985 = 0.115 m. There are no dry or unscored cells between pass
centers. Ideal alignment to the continuous towel edge at 1.050 m would give
0.215 m overlap; it is a different reference from the measured raster edge.
Finite-cell extents and partial-edge coverage explain the raster/analytic
differences; there is no claim that the physical towel became 2.400 m wide.

Wet aging
FreezeSeconds is an explicit exponential phase-change time constant, now
420 seconds instead of 85 seconds. It is a gameplay calibration, not a
temperature/slab/refrigeration calculation. For an initial 0.300 mm film:
  1 minute: 86.69 percent remains liquid.
  5 minutes: 48.95 percent remains liquid.
 15 minutes: 11.73 percent remains liquid.
 20 minutes:  5.74 percent remains liquid.
About 95 percent has frozen after 21 minutes. This lets earlier passes remain
visibly wet through much of a normal run while newer passes remain wetter.
The shader's continuous optical response is derived from that real liquid;
it is not an independent timer that silently preserves recoverable water.
Liquid plus frozen water remains constant during aging. Pickup can remove
only the actual liquid portion and still respects pump capacity and tank room.

Wear has physical depth
Every cell now retains InitialGougeDepthMm and remaining GougeDepthMm.
The seeded field includes coherent heavier-traffic patches and deeper wear
near sensitive goal areas. Its measured depth range is about 0.037-0.145 mm.
Defaults are MinimumGougeDepthMm=.024, TypicalGougeDepthMm=.060,
DeepGougeDepthMm=.145 and GougeRoughnessDepthMm=.080. The seed combines those
calibrations with random and coherent wear; minimum is a lower bound.

Actual effective blade bite reduces remaining groove depth. The existing
0.16 bite fraction remains: a .35 mm screw setting cuts .056 mm effectively,
while maximum .80 mm gives .128 mm. A shallow cut can leave a deep skate
gouge; a deeper pass or another appropriate pass removes more. More cutting
produces more conserved snow and retains existing load and overcut penalties.
Reverse cutting still removes material but adds surface damage. Washing
cannot make a physical groove disappear. Scratch is now a residual roughness
measure derived from depth, so the renderer's groove/roughness change follows
actual treatment rather than a color-only reset.

A paired pass with .15/.65 mm screw settings left mean residual depths of
.0251/.0006 mm over 11480 shared cut cells. Deeper cutting produced more snow.
Untreated ice is rendered dull and visibly worn, dry shaved ice has a distinct
appearance, and a real wet film adds the fresh sheen. Coverage and surface
quality remain separate: a pass can cover the sheet yet leave deeper defects.

Performance and evidence
Dirty-cell updates, active-wet tracking and pooled state arrays remain in use.
There is no full-grid physics loop each tick. Surface scoring now maintains
exact incremental counts and double-precision quality/water totals as cells
change. Independent full scans verify identical counts, sums and rounded
report values. Raw fixture invalidation still rebuilds the totals explicitly.
The compact snapshots add about 5.6 MB of fixed storage at the 5 cm setting.
A changed report measured 0.0130 ms with no managed allocation; the full core
suite measured 0.2494 ms per active fixed tick over 5000 ticks, also with no
managed allocation. These CPU-only figures exclude Unity, HUD, GPU and display.

Visual aging of already-wet cells processes at most 65536 cells per surface
refresh. Newly changed cells update immediately, and explicit full invalidation
refreshes every cell exactly. An old wet cell can therefore display an age
about 1.6 seconds behind the nominal survey cadence on a fully wetted sheet,
small relative to the 420-second phase constant. This bounds rendering work;
it does not delay fluid freezing, pickup, material accounting or scoring.

The 5 cm field contains four times as much data; its cost must be measured,
not described as free higher resolution. Use the final SurfaceServiceRefinement
player performance reports for frame-time comparisons, including the explicitly
seeded late_wet_sheet workload, and the rendered visual validation captures for
width, groove depth and aging. Core evidence is in Validation~/Results.txt and
Validation~/Ice-visual-refinement-results.txt.

The exact visible wet edge, groove contrast and chosen 420-second calibration
still need operator playtesting. Neither this model nor its values certify
the behavior of a particular resurfacer or rink refrigeration system.
