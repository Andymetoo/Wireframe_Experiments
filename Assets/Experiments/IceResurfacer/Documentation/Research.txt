ICE RESURFACER / TECHNICAL REFERENCE AND CALIBRATION
Research checked 25 October 2026. Fictional machine: AURORA R-54.

Historical source/calibration record. Manufacturer references remain useful;
the 28 October OperatorFreedom.txt and README supersede the former central
south gate, blanket handling restrictions, controls and scenario scoring.
Safe manufacturer procedures are reference guidance, not Free Simulation gates.

Reference provenance

The principal source is Zamboni Company's Model 546 Operating Instructions,
DM-54620, revision 03/12/2025. The manufacturer Owners Area lists that edition:
https://zamboni.com/owners-area/?folder=546

The full manufacturer-authored PDF was downloaded to a temporary research
directory and its text inspected; no manual, photographs or logos are bundled:
https://cdn4.sportngin.com/attachments/document/051a-3512134/546_Operating_Instructions_English.pdf

Manufacturer web chapters require a password. A manufacturer-authored 2020
manual mirrored at ManualsLib was initially cross-checked, then superseded by
the 2025 PDF above. The editions differ on maximum flood-water temperature;
the prototype does not simulate temperature or teach tank-filling procedure.

Physical reference values

Source: manufacturer Model 546 specification sheet, revision 06/2021, also
confirmed by the manufacturer's currently published product specification:

Tank lowered envelope: 4.04 m long, 2.13 m wide, 2.21 m high.
Tank raised envelope: 5.03 m long, 2.13 m wide, 3.73 m high.
Wheelbase: 1.96 m; wheel track: 1.37 m.
Turning radius measured at conditioner: 4.87 m.
Shaving blade: 1.956 m long; horizontal/vertical auger diameter: 0.254 m.
Flood-water capacity: 738 L; wash-water capacity: 310 L.
Snow tank: 2.83 cubic metres actual volume, 3.54 cubic metres compacted rating.

Hydrostatic transmission and hydrodynamic braking are authentic. These figures
guide scale and capacity; the original model is not a CAD replica.

Operating facts used by the prototype

References below are printed chapter/page numbers in the 2025 manual:

- 7-2: Releasing the accelerator causes hydrostatic braking; a separate foot
  brake is available. Recommended resurfacing engine speed: 2,400-2,800 RPM.
- 7-6: Enter with conditioner raised. While moving slowly forward, lower it
  with blade retracted, start conveyors, then adjust cut. No mandatory
  vertical-before-horizontal starting order is stated.
- 7-10 and 8-1: Open wash valve, wait at least ten seconds, then enable pickup
  pump. Water returns through a filter. Stop pump before closing valve.
- 7-11/12: Left brush sweeps kickplate snow before flooding. Reduce flood flow
  through repeated overlaps, corners and goal areas. Counterclockwise raises
  the blade handwheel; clockwise increases cut.
- 7-14/15: Retract blade, stop augers and pump, close water valves; fully lift
  conditioner after vertical auger stops. Dump off ice, stationary and level,
  with conveyors stopped. Lower tank before moving.
- 7-16: Deep cuts at high ground speed increase load.

Mechanism reference: https://zamboni.com/about/how-it-works/
Shaved ice travels through horizontal and vertical conveyors to the snow tank.
The wash circuit and the fresh ice-making-water circuit have different jobs.
A cloth spreader trails the conditioner and distributes the fresh water.

Prototype assumptions and calibration

The fictional 60 x 26 m rink has 8.5 m rounded corners, an open central south
service gate and an adjacent level service bay. These are scenario dimensions,
not Model 546 specifications. Nets are parked off the playing surface for the
resurfacing session. All spectators and players have already left the ice.

SimulatorConfig is the calibration authority. Its SI-valued fields are serializable
for playtesting. Manufacturer reference values above do not validate the following
chosen model parameters: acceleration/deceleration, speed limits, steering rate,
effective trailer pivot, lift timings, throughput, snow expansion, water recovery,
target water depth, freeze time, ice-cut thresholds, damage and scoring weights.

The 4.87 m conditioner radius is a calibration target. The bicycle model's rear
axle radius and the conditioner trajectory differ; changing wheelbase, steering
limit or hitch geometry requires a turning-circle check. The conditioner is a
lagging articulated footprint, not a mark painted beneath the vehicle centre.

The 3.54 cubic-metre capacity is treated as equivalent compacted snow capacity;
the spatial simulation converts shaved ice to equivalent snow using a tunable
expansion ratio. This is an approximation, not a compaction or granular-flow solver.

Each surface cell retains its own condition. Water quantity, cut and snow handling
affect finish instead of awarding credit merely for driving over a location.
The initial seeded wear, persistent cut depth and wet-to-frozen transition are
game models. There is no thermal ice-sheet, slab or refrigeration simulation.

Goal/crease sensitivity makes careful cut and water adjustment useful. Its stricter
cut threshold is deliberately a scenario rule, not a published manufacturer limit.
The raised-conditioner exit check represents clearance at the service threshold;
there is no structural blade-deformation simulation. Stationary dumping and tank
movement restrictions represent equipment handling without a dynamic rollover model.

W/S combine pedal demand and direction selection for keyboard play. The real
machine uses separate hydrostatic direction and engine-speed controls. The lower
centre mouse panel is an original accessible arrangement, not a traced dashboard.
Physical control animations communicate actual simulation state. The initial
engine warm-up, guard inspection and blade installation have already occurred.

This entertainment prototype is not an operator qualification or a substitute
for equipment-specific training. Asset names, graphics and AURORA branding are
original; Zamboni is cited only as the technical reference.
