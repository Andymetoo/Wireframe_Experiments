HOW THE CONTROLS DIFFER

Flood flow scales the actual finite supply at the existing valve position.
The Inspector shows effective full-valve L/s, predicted full-tank minutes and
measured consumption L/s. At 1.8 L/s, 738 L lasts 410 s (6:50); at 0.72 L/s it
lasts 1,025 s (17:05). This does not guarantee adequate film at every speed.

New shavings expansion changes only the bulk volume produced from newly
removed solid ice. The "2.5x loose volume" button changes 3.1 to 7.75 m3/m3.
Existing loose snow is picked up once at its existing volume. Tank inventory
is never independently multiplied. Expansion is a density calibration, not
extra solid ice removal. The default remains 1x until you choose an experiment.

Horizontal and vertical throughput scale finite conveyor capacities. A stopped
motor transports nothing; a full throat backs up; a crowded/full tank, raised
bin or jam can reject material through the existing physical discharge paths.
The trough threshold sets retained loose volume before actual overflow. A lower
threshold makes spillage begin earlier; lowering it below current stock sends
the excess to the existing side deposits on the next tick.

Bin visual fullness is presentation only. Its 2.5x button makes partial contents
look fuller, capped at the shell's existing fitted maximum. The physical tank
inventory and gameplay gauge continue to report actual stock. Zero stays empty.
Overflow density and clump size affect transient particles only; actual deposit
depth, location and material balances remain authoritative. No particles are
emitted at zero actual spill. Existing pool/emission limits still cap density.

Wash recovery uses the existing recirculation and surface-pickup model. It is
not a forced tank-loss setting. Pump capacity, actual available surface liquid,
residual film, priming, damage, freeze/spread and tank headroom still limit return.
It does not change wash supply or the treatment delivered to the ice.

Simulation controls have bounded sliders and finite-number validation. Physical
tank capacities remain 738 L flood, 310 L wash and 3.54 m3 loose-equivalent snow.
The Inspector meters separately show solid shaving, expanded new snow, existing
loose pickup, tank delivery, stocks, overflow and wash/flood supply and return.
Rates average at least 0.25 simulated seconds and freeze with simulation time.
No gameplay HUD gauges or engineering panels were added.

functional conflict the previous pass resolved by moving the model. That move
is not repeated here. Small plausible choices for a later decision are:

  1. Restore the preferred roller placement and let the true conditioner/body
     envelope contact first. Keep the simulation matched to the visual model;
     review protective side skids and resulting handling. No invisible roller.
  2. Accept a compact compromise: about x -.981 gives a 5 mm straight lead with
     the current .09 m radius. That is 154 mm outward from the earlier pose,
     or 20 mm inward from the present one. It cannot preserve the earlier pose.

Making the small guide lead from x -.827 would require roughly .239 m radius,
or a narrower conditioner envelope. Those are substantial mechanical redesigns,
not a small correction; neither is proposed for this pass. Turning also matters:
at +1 degree heading (nose away from the left boards), conditioner reach is
1.0913 m while current roller reach is 1.0482 m. Even today's straight lead does
not guarantee roller-first contact while turning. Full support measurements at
-5/-1/0/+1/+5 degrees are in asset-audit.json. Collision safety is unchanged.
Any later geometry change must retain strict route compatibility/revalidation.
