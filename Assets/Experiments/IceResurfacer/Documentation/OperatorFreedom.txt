AURORA R-54 / OPERATOR FREEDOM AND COORDINATED REFINEMENT
28 October 2026

Current launch and controls
Scenes/IceResurfacer.unity remains the entry point. The root README is the full
current control guide. This document supersedes the control bindings, central
south service location, blanket dismount/raised-tank/hose travel restrictions,
and earlier score weights described in the 25-27 October historical documents.
Those documents retain their original measurements and failure reproduction.

Mode separation and input authority
SimulationMode.FreeSimulation is the default for both scored and practice
sessions. Practice determines whether a score is displayed; it does not decide
which machinery actions are allowed. GuidedTraining is a distinct mode that
adds explicit safety assistance. The welcome panel names all three choices.
This is an assisted practice mode with an operator guide, not a new multi-stage
scripted curriculum. Training-only reasons identify themselves in the UI.

OperatorControls remains the single machinery command state. Keyboard inputs,
physical controls and HUD write the same valve openings, auger states, blade
setting and hydraulic spool commands. Held hydraulic controls spring to neutral
on release, focus/pause or dismount; the attained cylinder position remains.
A released mouse drag cannot capture driving input. F3 reports requested pedal,
accepted demand, brake/contact, actual tank state, hose, focus and cursor state.
The original rejected-target drive latch remains a regression case in assisted
mode; Free Simulation does not use tank elevation or a connected hose as drive
interlocks. On foot, the absent pedal is released into hydrostatic coasting.

Free actions and physical consequences
A player can operate conveyors, valves, brush, conditioner and dump hydraulics
away from the rink or pit; dismount while moving/on ice/with equipment active;
and drive with a raised tank or connected hose. No action moves the machine
into a preferred dock pose. Actual wall/pit obstruction, hydraulic travel,
conveyor jam/capacity and pump condition remain meaningful mechanical limits.

Raised-tank cornering reduces grip and records misuse. Cutting/transport overload
can jam or spill snow. Dumping follows the actual moving outlet and gravity-
inspired fall, with lateral distribution across the opening. Material goes to
the pit, spatial ice cells or the persistent floor map according to landing
geometry. Residual shakeout still requires actual lower/raise motion and uses
the same outlet. Overflows are not counted twice as stored snow. Unconventional
techniques are judged by resulting conditions, not conformity to a route.

Dismount chooses clear ground around the machine, avoiding walls/pit/body, and
keeps existing machinery state. The moving vehicle may coast after the operator
leaves it. Accessible rear-platform boarding provides recovery when a wall
blocks the usual left step. A carried nozzle may be left on the floor when
boarding and recovered with E. Exceeding attached hose reach releases a fictional
breakaway coupling, spills live supply and reports a fault; it does not freeze
travel. There is no vehicle teleport or whole-session reset in these recoveries.

Camera and reference geometry
Q targets the actual left conditioner/brush geometry; E targets the inboard
control station; C looks back across the fresh strip. Presets use a smooth
approximately 0.2-second transition, persist when released, and can be continued
with RMB look. R toggles stance, Space temporarily stands, and X returns forward.
Comma/period replace the former Q/E blade controls. Standing does not recenter.
The pause panel provides -8 to +12 cm eye-height adjustment. On foot, ordinary
mouse movement controls view and E interacts through the center aim. Capture
releases for pause/guide and returns to the ordinary seated cursor on boarding.

The supplied traditional hydraulic-station image and actual tall seated-view
photo are the current reference pair. The inboard control bank, compact valve
area, tapered/beveled forward body, console transition and operator sightlines
use those references. The described conditioner annotation and board-gap images
were not attached; no assertion of matching those missing views is made.
Machine geometry is original and stays within the existing fictional scale;
manufacturer specifications inform proportions, not an exact CAD replica.

Surface geometry, recovery and scoring
The blade remains 1.956 m, water towel 2.100 m, body 2.130 m, wheel track 1.370 m,
and tire width 0.300 m. The outside tire edge is therefore 0.835 m from center.
The two-pass test found no foot-wide physical dry strip in the captured previous
straight-pass implementation. It also finds no dry/unscored gap after the new
finite lateral water fringe. The new strong wet band measures 2.2 m at the
specific 0.1 m grid phase, versus 2.0 m before; the low-volume fringe is wider.
This improves the visible edge without enlarging the real blade or adding free
water. Perspective, turning/articulation and which boundary a player follows
still need human calibration. IceCalibration.txt gives the exact thresholds.

A configurable 0.1524 m shaving exclusion follows the rounded boards. Combined
coverage/shaving use the reachable mask; water/finish still measure the complete
sheet. F2 cycles normal, shaving-mask, and actual cut/wash/flood channel views.
Cells outside the rink do not receive lateral film through boards. Outside-
rink discharge goes into the separate spatial floor material map.

Flood water has distinct liquid and frozen quantities. A primed, functioning
pickup pump can collect only liquid within the real squeegee contact strip,
subject to pump capacity, filtering and available wash-tank space. Recovery
returns measured liters to that reservoir and retains a thin residual film.
Frozen water is not erased. Overwater defects retain their frozen component;
only actual recovery improves the liquid contribution. Sequential cut, pickup
and trailing towel footprints follow the machine's actual contact geometry.
The board brush transfers snow only within its deployed disc, producing a
nearby windrow for the conditioner rather than awarding automatic coverage.

The results panel separates coverage, surface quality, water distribution,
excess water, missed patches, water-use efficiency, tank/return handling and
misuse. Point weights are 40 + 30 + 10 + 10 + 5 + 5, less recorded misuse.
The prior 95.18% / 85.2-point run is a historical baseline, not a claim that the
new weights and mask should yield an identical score. Current route results
and their comparison are recorded in the generated validation reports.

The control-driven southeast full run in Southeast-full-run.txt reaches
97.05% reachable combined coverage, or 95.16% when measured against ALL ice
cells for comparison with the prior 95.18% baseline. It records 97.25% water
coverage, 82.2% finish, 90.4 points, 674.8 L flood use, 0.723 m3 in the pit,
1759 simulated seconds, zero misuse faults and a proper return. It never
teleports the machine or edits cells/tanks during the route. The changed mask
and score weights explain why the current score is not directly comparable.
The unconventional session finishes without reset at 2.37% coverage / 10.5
points, with seven faults, tracked floor water and 0.01350 m3 dumped onto ice.
Low results describe its actual misuse and limited treatment, not a route ban.

Performance work and evidence
The F4 FPS/frame-time meter is visible by default and refreshes text at 4 Hz.
F3 adds measured update CPU work, GPU timing when the platform supplies it,
last-frame GC allocation and draw-call counters. It also breaks Estimated
Machine Load into cutting, conveyors/augers and congestion contributions.

Surface change tracking avoids regenerating untouched channels; active wet
cells alone update fading, unchanged textures skip upload, and surface score
totals are cached by revision. Floor deposits use two bounded meshes updated
at 5 Hz only when material state changes. Hose routing uses reusable arrays;
water/snow pools are bounded. There are no per-cell scene objects. These code
changes are not themselves proof of a frame-rate improvement: use
Performance.txt for measured before/after player results, resolutions, scope,
profiler availability and limitations.

The aggregate core harness passed all 32 scenario groups, recorded in
Validation~/Results.txt. Full runtime and hidden-player performance
results are separate checks; the control-driven route alone does not prove them.
Validation~/Freedom-results.txt covers mode isolation, free operation, spatial
dumps, traction, manual southeast entry/exit and unconventional use without a
reset. Ice-calibration-results.txt and Ice-width-before-results.txt measure
world-space tire alignment, board fringe, shaving mask and liquid recovery.
Service-results.txt covers actual Input System F/E/W walking around the machine,
independent fills/overflow, broad highlighted return, restored cursor/camera,
driven hose breakaway and moving active-equipment dismount. Camera, physical
controls, operation and native HUD reports provide the complementary checks.
Only the final generated reports establish pass/fail status; documentation
should not substitute for running the supplied harnesses.

Remaining limits and operator checks
This is a deterministic kinematic simulator with mild slip, not a full tire,
suspension or rollover solver. Maximum forward speed is configurable and now
2.65 m/s (9.54 km/h); acceleration/hydrostatic braking remain calibrated.
There is no injury simulation, thermal slab model, granular snow compaction,
full fluid solver, dynamic hose self-collision, or maintenance workshop repair.
Pump wear is restored by a new session, not magically by filling water.
Audio is synthesized rather than machine recordings.

Please assess Q/E sightlines and mouse reach at both stances, tire-to-strip
alignment through real turns, doorway/pit approaches, modest traction at
ordinary versus abrupt steering, wet-floor appearance, hose weight/return feel
and sound balance. Automated geometry and input checks constrain the design;
they do not replace the former operator's judgment.
