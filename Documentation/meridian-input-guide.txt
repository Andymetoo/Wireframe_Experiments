MERIDIAN 1944 — INPUT AND SQUADRON CONTROLS

The keyboard and mouse flight controls are retained. W/S lower/raise the nose,
A/D bank, Q/E rudder, Shift/Ctrl or R/F adjust throttle, Space or left mouse
fires, and Z levels the aircraft using control inputs. C changes view; G toggles
the lead cue; M enables the original mouse stick. Tab selects the next target.
Hold right mouse to look around; V or middle mouse centers the view. Look never
changes the aircraft's flight attitude or the direction of its guns.
In mouse-stick mode, RMB temporarily withholds mouse steering and releasing RMB
recenters the cursor before mouse steering resumes. Keyboard steering still works.

Squadron orders: 1 Form Up; 2 Engage selected target; 3 Cover Me; 4 Regroup.
Changing the selected target does not silently change a previous attack order.
Issue Engage again to designate a different contact to your wingmen.

GAMEPAD (Xbox names / physical button positions)
Left stick: roll and pitch. Pull down to raise the nose.
Triggers: left/right rudder. RB: guns. LB: level assist.
D-pad up/down: throttle. Right stick: look; click to center.
X (west): target. B (east): view. A (south): lead cue. Start: pause.
Hold Y (north), then D-pad: up Form Up; right Engage; left Cover Me; down Regroup.
While issuing orders, D-pad input does not move the throttle.
Menus: D-pad up/down moves focus; A selects; B goes back.
The mission briefing lists five choices; select a mission, then focus Take the Air.

Pause settings include flight sensitivity, analog deadzone, look speed, analog
pitch/roll/rudder inversion, mouse-stick pitch inversion, and look inversion.
Analog pitch and roll are read before Unity's default gamepad deadzone processor
so the configured deadzone is applied once. Releasing a stick centers the pilot's
control demand, not the aircraft: angular momentum remains in the flight model.
Unplugging a controller clears its held controls; the last throttle setting stays.

OPTIONAL JOYSTICK / HOTAS BINDING EXTENSION
The pause menu's Flight-stick Bindings panel accepts Unity Input System control
paths for individual roll, pitch, rudder, throttle and fire controls. Example axis:
<Joystick>/stick/x. Actual paths depend on the installed device layout. Empty fields
remain unbound. Use a single axis (/x or /y), not a complete Vector2 stick. Apply
the fields to activate them. A throttle can span either -1..+1 or 0..+1.
Binding a whole Vector2 stick to a single axis safely disables that binding and
shows an error. Invalid throttle bindings preserve the last commanded throttle.

Bindings use scene-owned InputActions and are disposed when leaving Air Combat.
No global input settings, device disabling, or other game's handlers are changed.
Settings persist across sorties within Air Combat, not across leaving the mode or
quitting the executable. This is an extensible binding path, not a claim of universal
joystick/HOTAS compatibility. Specialized devices require actual hardware testing.

IMPLEMENTATION / VALIDATION
AirInputAdapter converts devices to AirControlIntent (FlightInput, look, orders,
menu commands). AirCombatGame applies level assistance, commands and camera intent;
AirMission and FlightDynamics know nothing about keys, mice or controllers.
AirInputValidation is opt-in via the cabinet diagnostic walkthrough. It injects
states into a temporary real Unity InputSystem Gamepad and verifies neutral input,
simultaneous controls, deadzones, axis inversion, throttle, command chords, menu
buttons, an InputAction control-path binding and disconnection. The helper releases
its malformed-binding fixtures after checking one-time disabling; temporary mouse
input verifies that freelook does not also steer the mouse stick. It releases
its temporary device and bindings without persisting global input settings.
Execution results are reported separately by the walkthrough; source coverage alone
is not a passed test. Physical gamepad and specialized joystick comfort/compatibility
remain human hardware validation work.
