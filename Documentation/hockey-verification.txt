SIMULATION AND PLAY
Skating uses acceleration, retained momentum, limited turning/edge forces and braking.
Looking does not rotate existing velocity. The puck slides freely and is guided by a
capped stick force within reach, never parented to a skater. Shots and passes impart
velocity; board, post, net, player and pad contacts produce deflections and rebounds.
Fast pucks use collision substeps. Stick interaction cannot reach through netting.
Team pilots distinguish retrieval, carrier pressure, passing support and defensive
cover. Retrievers navigate around goal nets and reach the rounded boards. Goalies
track shooting angles after a 0.23-0.35 second reaction interval and make physical saves.
Human and AI players use the same skating, striking and contact rules.

PRACTICAL LIMITS / HUMAN PLAYTESTING
The puck is simulated on the ice plane; there are no aerial/lifted shots, icing,
offsides, penalties, line changes, stamina or player switching. The human stays Blue #9.
The visual puck is deliberately a little larger than its collision disc.
Statistics count pass releases and physical events, not full regulation scorekeeping.
Keyboard/mouse feel, comfort, shot difficulty, teammate usefulness and long-term fun
still need human playtesting. Automated bot outcomes do not establish those qualities.

HOW TO PLAY
Run Builds/VectorCabinet/VectorCabinet.exe beside its data folder and DLLs; choose Hockey
or press 3. Open Ice practice explains the mechanics without a running clock.
Mouse looks/aims; WASD pushes; Shift accelerates; Space brakes. Hold/release LMB shoots,
F quick-shoots, RMB/Q passes, E checks and R requests a pass. C changes camera; Esc
pauses and provides restart and return-to-cabinet controls. README.md has the full guide.
