MERIDIAN 1944: SQUADRON AND TACTICAL WARFARE
Implementation and numerical validation notes, 2026-10-12

SCOPE AND PRESERVATION

The existing Air Combat game is extended inside the three-game collection. The
original Duel and Convoy Cover missions keep their original generation, legacy
pilot-update path, and outcome rules. FlightDynamics.cs is unchanged. No new
packages, creative assets, textures, audio, network service, or Unity project is
required. Haunted House and Hockey gameplay are outside this change's scope.
Unity runtime, rendering, launcher regression and standalone build verification
are separate from the numerical evidence below and were pending when this file
was first written. Consult the final expansion verification report for those.

NEW SORTIES

Each new mission gives Kestrel two mortal wingmen, Rook and Finch, with 1,100
rounds each. Kestrel has 1,500 rounds. They begin in a loose formation and follow
orders using the same aerodynamic forces as the player. Mission seeds reproduce
initial headings, forces, attack directions and the pilots' random decisions.

Interception:
  Two raid leaders are outbound along a known route, with one or two fighter
  escorts. Both leaders must be stopped before either crosses the defended
  boundary. Destroying escort fighters alone cannot win. The route is about
  35 km, usually about five minutes to the defended boundary; actual elapsed
  time depends on flight, evasion and interception. The longer route permits
  recovery after an initial head-on pass without changing enemy performance.
  Pilot guidance aims beyond the finish plane, avoiding circles around a point
  immediately before the boundary. Search guidance uses the briefing's route
  and estimated cruise speed, not an omniscient update of hostile positions.
  Split attacks can let the player engage one raider while wingmen pressure the
  other. Chasing escorts can allow the raid to get through.

Bomber Escort:
  Three Heron B.26 bombers fly a physical formation for roughly 19 km. At least
  two must actually cross the destination plane while the player survives.
  Three hostile fighters preferentially attack bombers. Killing every hostile
  does not replace the arrival requirement; losing two bombers before arrival
  makes the mission impossible and causes defeat. The bomber leader flies a
  route, followers use local formation slots and fall back to their route if
  the leader dies. Bombers have 195 health, larger swept collision shapes and
  slower transport-like performance. Their models use generated geometry.

Ground Attack:
  Four moving supply trucks are the required objectives. Two finite-ammunition
  flak emplacements and two defending fighters are optional threats. Trucks
  follow the terrain at 2.4 m/s. Guns fire real projectiles; three 13-damage
  aircraft rounds disable a 39-health truck. Flak has 65 health and emits
  imperfectly led 7-damage rounds in limited bursts, 440 rounds per gun.
  Destroying the four trucks while alive wins, even if fighters or flak remain.
  There is no hidden extraction or fighter kill requirement. Fuel ends an
  unfinished ground sortie at ten minutes; other tactical sorties are bounded
  to eight minutes if their normal objective has not ended them sooner.

TACTICAL PILOTS AND ORDERS

Form Up, Engage, Cover Me and Regroup change persistent mission intent. A
designated contact can be an aircraft or ground vehicle. Merely looking at the
next target does not silently change a previously issued attack order. Missions
start in Form Up and reset order state on restart. Wingmen acknowledge commands
in text; surviving pilots retain immediate terrain, speed and threat priorities.

The tactical pilot layers intentions onto the existing bank/elevator controller:
intercept, attack, defensive break, extend, energy climb, low-speed recovery,
terrain escape, damaged/empty-gun withdrawal, search, formation and rejoin. It
uses delayed decisions, target commitment, imperfect bursts, bounded visual
awareness and terrain occlusion. Formation control adjusts speed and bank to a
leader-relative slot rather than attaching aircraft to the leader. Aircraft
positions, velocity and rotation still come from FlightDynamics.

Strafing pilots line up a run, require suitable orientation, lead the moving
vehicle with gravity compensation, fire physical rounds and pull out through
actual lift before returning for another pass. A destroyed target does not
cancel a needed recovery maneuver. Friendly firing solutions avoid allies;
friendly projectiles retain the pre-existing team damage filter.

WEAPONS, DAMAGE AND SESSION LIFETIME

Weapons still use muzzle velocity plus shooter velocity, finite ammunition,
gravity, dispersion, round lifetime and swept moving-target collision. The same
round path now tests terrain and ground targets before applying damage. Only
rounds owned by player ID 0 count toward the player's hit tally; wingman hits
cannot inflate it. Ground flak rounds are reported separately from aircraft gun
rounds. Destroyed targets stop moving/firing and remain as renderable wrecks.

New tactical missions also test swept conservative fuselage-core collisions.
Both colliding aircraft are destroyed; no avoidance code teleports them apart.
All simulation state belongs to an AirMission instance. A completed mission
stops advancing. No new static match state or event subscription is needed.

AUTOMATED EVIDENCE AT IMPLEMENTATION HANDOFF

Command, from ValidationAir:
  dotnet run --project AirCombat.Validation.csproj --configuration Release

MissionValidation covers 72 generated tactical forces; unique IDs and safe
spawns; correct objective counts; command/restart state; invalid time deltas;
moving swept ground hits and misses; physical truck and wingman destruction;
flak ammunition/burst limits; objective precedence; projectile-to-goal outcome
integration; physical aircraft collision; deterministic repeated state; and
complete, unmodified bot sorties. It asserts at least one actual bot victory
for each new mission kind across its three diagnostic seeds. Defeats are also
reported; it does not require every AI sortie to win.

Latest .NET complete bot sorties at handoff (no position, health or ammo edits):
  Interception 1944: victory 236.4 s, 2/2 raiders, player 8 impacts.
  Interception 81: defeat 209.0 s, player shot down, 0/2 raiders.
  Interception 31337: defeat 303.6 s, 1/2 raiders, player 3 impacts.
  Bomber Escort 1944: victory 204.6 s, 2 arrivals, all 3 bombers alive.
  Bomber Escort 81: victory 212.4 s, 2 arrivals, 1 bomber lost.
  Bomber Escort 31337: defeat 162.9 s, 2 bombers lost, route 76% complete.
  Ground Attack 1944: victory 237.9 s, 4/4 trucks; Rook shot down.
  Ground Attack 81: victory 306.5 s, 4/4 trucks; Finch shot down.
  Ground Attack 31337: defeat 257.4 s, 1/4 trucks; player and Finch shot down.
  No aircraft collisions occurred in these nine full sorties.

TacticalValidationPilot is an additional controls-only expert regression driver,
not a player assist. It selects raid targets and sends legal squadron orders,
then issues bounded pitch, roll, yaw, throttle and fire inputs. It never edits
the simulation state. Its split-target interception seed 81 earned victory in
163.9 s with 98 player rounds and 8 impacts. Seed 31337 won in 256.0 s with
76 player rounds and 16 impacts, but both wingmen were lost. Seed 1944 lost at
the boundary despite landing hits. Original ValidationPilot remains unchanged.

Existing legacy dogfight diagnostics still reported 6/6 physical-gunnery wins
in 83.7-221.3 seconds and 3/3 armed courier-escort wins.

The full .NET harness passed 776 checks with zero failures at handoff, including
the tactical handling fixtures. Head-on aircraft physically passed at 38 metres
after both selected the conventional right-side avoidance maneuver. The terrain
recovery fixture climbed from a minimum 94 to 389 metres AGL. These are numerical
results, not a substitute for final Unity/build verification. Exact seeded
trajectories may differ between .NET and Unity floating-point implementations;
repeatability is tested within each runtime, not assumed across runtimes.

LIMITS OF THIS EVIDENCE

These tests demonstrate reachable outcomes, physical gunnery, finite ammunition,
route completion, mortality and bounded simulation. They do not establish human
control comfort, visual readability, enjoyable balance, or squadron personality.
Those require actual player sessions. The player still has to manage speed,
lead shots, choose orders and watch the mission assets. There are no turreted
bomber guns, bombing subsystem, campaign, radio audio or perfect tactical radar.
