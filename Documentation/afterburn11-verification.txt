WHAT CHANGED

Flight usability changes apply to standard missions and Afterburn:
- Hold RMB to look around independently of the airframe; release returns forward.
  Head limits reach 165 degrees either side. Hold X for a quick rear-hemisphere
  view. X takes precedence over RMB; V/middle mouse still centers immediately.
  Mouse-stick steering and mouse gunfire are withheld during the look gesture;
  releasing safely recenters the cursor before mouse steering resumes.
- Tactical slot 1: T or 5. Slot 2: Y or 6. Gamepad: hold Y, then LB/RB.
  The two device families are labeled separately. HUD cards show charges, active
  duration, and concise unavailable reasons. Existing throttle controls remain.
- Floating label backplates are replaced by stable colored text with a dark
  outline. The gunsight is stronger and outlined; lead guidance defaults on but
  G still toggles it. None of this aims the guns or changes projectile collisions.
- A brief X confirms actual player-caused aircraft damage. Amber destruction
  feedback is separate. Ground hits, missed rounds, and reticle overlap cannot
  increment the aircraft-hit counter.
- Finalized outcomes receive a three-second visual aftermath. Geometric wrecks
  coast, descend and trail smoke; player destruction uses an external camera.
  Authoritative time, damage, ammunition, score and outcome remain frozen.
  Enter/gamepad A skips; pause, binding menus, restart and exit remain available.
- All surviving critical vehicles have individual V1–V4 markers and radar
  diamonds, including directional guidance outside view. Raiders and the ace
  have objective guidance; the larger battle marks its contested sector.
  Unrelated hidden fighters are not revealed. Destroyed objectives disappear.
- Equipment, route and ability descriptions use larger type and adjusted card
  spacing. Layout checks cover generated descriptions, stacked kits and stages.

MISSIONS AND PROGRESSION

The original five missions remain. Standard briefing keys 7 and 8 add Ace Hunt
and Channel Air Battle; 6 still opens Afterburn.

Ace Hunt pits the player's flight against Voss and escorts. The ace uses stock
health, engine and aerodynamic limits. His advantages are quicker decisions,
disciplined bursts, defensive reversals and refusing a losing climb. Later
versions add a second escort and a greater starting altitude advantage.

Channel Air Battle adds an independent friendly Blue pair alongside Rook and
Finch. Defeat three of five hostile fighters across two flights; the finale asks
for four of seven across three flights. Every scheduled flight must physically
reach the contested sector, or be destroyed, before victory. Reinforcements
start outside friendly visual/combat bubbles and fly into the engagement. Pilots
commit to a return into the sector rather than repeatedly reversing at its edge.
Fruitless sustained circles prompt an energy extension and another approach.
These Fleet-only decisions still use the ordinary bounded flight controls.

The old operation used overlapping mission pools and hidden enemy stat pressure.
The revised run communicates its four stages explicitly:
1. Opening moves: duel, courier protection or a risky ground strike.
2. Contested skies: bomber escort, raider interception or the first ace encounter.
3. Pressure front: reinforced ground attack, two-flight battle or escorted ace.
4. Final offensive: reinforced bomber protection, three-flight battle or ace
   with two escorts and the greater altitude advantage.
Escalation uses encounter structure rather than added enemy hull or gun damage.
The existing equipment, supplies, repairs, wingman losses and tactical cards
remain the run's economy. There is still no persistent save or permanent grind.

INTERCEPTION FINDINGS

The previous setup forced an early head-on merge followed by a large reversal
into a tail chase. The new diagonal crossing starts the raid 150 m below the
player; escorts approach ahead on the flank. Aircraft definitions, raid speed
capabilities and the defended-route objective are retained. Briefings explain
cutting across the raid's route and splitting targets with wingmen.

The before/after audit used six fixed seeds with ordinary and controls-only
expert pilots. Earned wins improved from 4/12 to 6/12; the expert sample improved
from 3/6 to 5/6. Ordinary pilots fired at roughly 33–35 seconds in all six new
starts. Boundary failures still occur; this is an opportunity and communication
improvement, not a guarantee of victory or a claim about human difficulty.
Logs: Logs/Afterburn11/interception-before.txt and interception-after.txt.

HUMAN PLAYTESTING STILL NEEDED

Automated victories prove the physical objectives and state transitions can
complete; they do not establish enjoyment or ideal balance. Please assess rear
view comfort, free-look with a physical mouse/controller, readability on your
display, interception pressure, ace fairness, larger-battle pacing and the
four-sortie difficulty curve. In the broad automated sample, stock final battles
won only 1/18 trials; the legally upgraded campaign won its final battle. This
remains the clearest balance question for human playtesting, not proof that an
unmodified aircraft has a comfortable finale. Specialized flight-stick/HOTAS hardware
and a formal GPU/frame-time benchmark have not been tested in this pass.
